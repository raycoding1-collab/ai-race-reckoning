An interactive teardown

The machine behind the machine.

Every frontier AI model is trained on packages like this one: one slab of logic walled in by stacked memory, drawing a kilowatt around the clock. Scroll to take it apart.

Scroll
01 / 06  Compute die

787 mm² of logic.

One slab of silicon, as large as a lithography scanner can print in a single exposure. Matrix engines fill the two halves; the band across the middle is cache that keeps them fed.

  • 3 nm-class process
  • Roughly 200 billion transistors
  • 24.7 × 31.9 mm, at the reticle limit
02 / 06  High-bandwidth memory

Memory, stacked eight high.

Six towers of DRAM flank the die. Each layer is thinned to about 30 µm and wired straight through the silicon, because moving data, rather than the arithmetic itself, sets the pace of most AI work.

  • 6 stacks × 8 DRAM dies
  • 144 GB in total
  • About 7.2 TB/s combined
03 / 06  Silicon interposer

Millimetres, not centimetres.

Logic and memory sit side by side on a slab of passive silicon wired like a miniature circuit board. Tens of thousands of microbumps join them, so signals cross millimetres instead of centimetres of board.

  • 46 × 35 mm of silicon
  • Microbumps at about 45 µm pitch
  • Thousands of wires per millimetre
04 / 06  Decoupling capacitors

440 tiny reservoirs.

Current demand swings by hundreds of amps within microseconds as work starts and stops. Ceramic capacitors ring the die and hold its voltage steady while it does.

  • 368 on top, 72 underneath
  • 0402 size: 1.0 × 0.5 mm
  • Multilayer ceramic (MLCC)
05 / 06  Ball grid array

3,456 ways down.

Every watt and every signal reaches the board through a grid of solder balls. Most of them carry power and ground: at under one volt, a kilowatt means more than a thousand amps.

  • 3,456 balls at 1.0 mm pitch
  • More than 1,000 A of supply current
06 / 06  Under load

1,000 watts, all day.

Nearly every joule the die spends on arithmetic leaves it as heat. At this power, fans give way to liquid cold plates, and the building around the chip is designed for water as much as for electricity.

  • About 1 kW per package
  • Direct liquid cooling is typical
