Chapter IV

The Fakes

Synthetic faces and voices have become cheap enough to aim at anyone: a finance clerk, a retiree, a teenager.

8mdeepfakes shared in 2025, by the British government's estimate, up from 500,000 in 2023

In January 2024 an employee in the Hong Kong office of Arup, the British engineering firm, joined a video call with the company's chief financial officer and several colleagues. Every other person on the call was a deepfake. By the time the fraud was discovered, the employee had made 15 transfers totaling HK$200 million, about US$25.6 million.{{fn:arup}}

The Arup case made headlines because of its size. Most cases are smaller and more personal. In France, a woman identified only as Anne lost €830,000 to scammers who posed as the actor Brad Pitt, using AI-generated images to sustain the romance.{{fn:pitt}} The cybersecurity company Surfshark has tallied $1.56 billion in reported losses to deepfake fraud through September 2025, more than $1 billion of it in 2025 alone.{{fn:surfshark-deepfake}}

Each dot is 20,000 deepfakes. In 2023, by the British government's estimate, about 500,000 were shared online.

By 2025 the estimate was 8 million.

Many deepfakes are sexual, and many of their targets are children. A 2025 survey by Thorn, a nonprofit that builds technology against child sexual abuse, found that 1 in 17 young people aged 13 to 20 had been the subject of deepfake nudes, and that 1 in 8 knew someone who had.{{fn:thorn}} The United States made publishing such images a federal crime in May 2025, and since this May platforms have had to remove them within 48 hours of a victim's request.{{fn:takeitdown}}

The damage outlasts any single image. When anything can be faked, real evidence becomes deniable too. The legal scholars Robert Chesney and Danielle Citron called this the "liar's dividend": the benefit that accrues to anyone caught on camera, once the public has learned that cameras can lie.{{fn:chesney-citron}} In February, Britain announced what it called a world-first framework for testing tools that detect deepfakes.{{fn:uk-deepfakes}} Detection has become a race of its own.

Fraud and forgery cost money and reputations. The next cost falls on work itself.
