Chapter I

The Money

Four companies, one year, $725 billion. Who is building the track, and who will own it.

$725bnplanned 2026 capital spending by Amazon, Alphabet, Microsoft and Meta, combined

In February, when Amazon, Alphabet, Microsoft and Meta told investors how much they expected to spend in 2026, the figures added up to somewhere between $650 billion and $700 billion.{{fn:capex-yahoo,capex-cnbc}} By late summer, after each had raised its forecast at least once, their combined plans stood at roughly $725 billion: 77 percent more than the four had spent in 2025, which was itself a record.{{fn:capex-toms}}

Most of that money buys physical things: accelerator chips, the servers built around them, the halls that house them and the substations that feed them. And most of it ends up with one supplier. Analysts estimate that Nvidia sells the large majority of the chips used to train and run AI systems, more than 90 percent by some counts.{{fn:nvidia-cnbc,nvidia-share}} In October 2025 it became the first company in history to be worth $5 trillion.{{fn:nvidia-5t}}

Each square is one billion dollars. In 2025 the four companies spent about $410 billion on buildings, chips and equipment, a record at the time.

For 2026, their combined plans come to about $725 billion. The new squares are the increase: roughly $315 billion in a single year.

Amazon alone expects to spend about $200 billion.

The spending does not stop with the four. OpenAI says it has signed about $1.4 trillion of computing commitments over roughly eight years, with Oracle, Microsoft, Amazon, Google and CoreWeave among its partners.{{fn:openai-14t}} Its Stargate venture with Oracle and SoftBank was announced at the White House in January 2025 as a $500 billion, 10-gigawatt program; by that September the partners had lined up sites for nearly seven gigawatts.{{fn:stargate}} A gigawatt is roughly the output of a typical nuclear reactor.{{fn:doe-gw}}

Money on this scale does more than build machines. It decides who gets to compete. A company that wants to train a leading model now needs chips by the warehouse, electricity by the gigawatt and a balance sheet able to absorb years of losses. That narrows the field to a handful of American and Chinese firms and the governments behind them, and it gives those firms a direct interest in how quickly anyone else can slow them down.
