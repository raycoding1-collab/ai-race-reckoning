Chapter III

The Feed

Most traffic on the web now comes from software. A growing share of what people read there is written by software, too.

53%of all web traffic in 2025 was generated by bots

For years, "dead internet theory" was a joke told on forums: the idea that the web had quietly emptied of people, and that the accounts and articles left behind were mostly talking to each other. The measurements have started to look less like a joke.

Imperva, a security company that has tracked automated traffic for more than a decade, reported that bots generated 51 percent of all web traffic in 2024, the first time in ten years that machines outnumbered people. Thirty-seven percent of all traffic came from what the company calls bad bots, used for scraping, account takeovers and fraud.{{fn:imperva-2025}} In 2025 the automated share passed 53 percent.{{fn:imperva-2026}}

What those machines leave behind is changing as well. In April 2026, researchers from Stanford University, Imperial College London and the Internet Archive published a study that ran 33 months of archived web pages through an AI-text detector. Before ChatGPT's release in November 2022, AI-written sites were close to nonexistent. By mid-2025, about 35 percent of newly published websites were generated or assisted by AI, and 17.6 percent appeared to be written entirely by it.{{fn:ai-web-404,ai-web-decrypt}}

The study was more careful than the headlines about it. The researchers did not find a significant decline in factual accuracy. What they found was sameness: AI-written pages were less varied in what they said and noticeably more upbeat in how they said it.{{fn:ai-web-404}} A web that sounds cheerfully alike is a smaller problem than a web full of lies, but it comes with a feedback loop. Future models are trained on the web that current models are writing, and experiments published in Nature in 2024 found that models trained repeatedly on their own output gradually lose the rarer parts of what they once knew.{{fn:nature-collapse}}

Sameness is the mild version. The sharper one is impersonation.
