IIIAsuka and Nara538 – 794

奈良

The Buddha and the Law

A new faith from Korea, a state on the Chinese model, a capital of straight streets, and a Buddha sixteen metres tall.

In the middle of the sixth century King Seong of Baekje, on the Korean peninsula, sent the Yamato court an image of the Buddha and a set of sutras. The Nihon Shoki dates the gift to 552; other sources give 538. Neither date is secure, but the arrival of Buddhism through diplomacy in these years is.{{fn:buddhism}}

Prince Shōtoku and the rising sun

Tradition gives the new order a founder: Prince Shōtoku, regent for Empress Suiko. The Nihon Shoki credits him with a Seventeen-Article Constitution in 604, a set of moral precepts drawn from Buddhism and Confucius rather than a constitution in the modern sense. Many scholars now doubt he wrote it.{{fn:seventeen}} Temples were built in his time. Hōryū-ji, founded in 607, burned in 670 and was rebuilt by 711; its buildings of the late seventh and early eighth centuries are among the oldest wooden buildings in the world, and in 1993 they were among the first Japanese sites on the World Heritage List.{{fn:horyuji}}

Also in 607, the envoy Ono no Imoko carried a letter to the emperor of Sui China. It opened with the words at the top of this page, from “the Son of Heaven where the sun rises” to “the Son of Heaven where the sun sets”. It is the earliest known use of a phrase meaning land of the rising sun for Japan. Emperor Yang, who expected tribute from a lesser kingdom, was offended by the claim to equal rank.{{fn:sui-letter}}

A state on the Chinese model

In 645 Prince Naka no Ōe and Nakatomi no Kamatari, the founder of the Fujiwara house, had Soga no Iruka, the most powerful man at court, cut down in front of the empress. The Taika Reforms that followed began to remake Yamato as a centralized state on the model of Tang China.{{fn:isshi}} Taika, “great change”, was the first of Japan’s era names; there have been 248 of them since, and chapter XIII can tell you which one any year belongs to.{{fn:gengo}} By the end of the century the ruler had the title 天皇 tennō, heavenly sovereign, and the country the name 日本 Nihon, origin of the sun.{{fn:asuka}} The Taihō Code of 701 gave the state its laws.{{fn:taiho}}

In 710 the court moved to Heijō-kyō, the capital we call Nara. It was laid out on a grid after Chang’an, the Tang capital, though without its walls, and at its height it held between 50,000 and 100,000 people.{{fn:heijo}} Here the court had its past written down. The Kojiki, “record of ancient matters”, was presented to Empress Genmei in 712. Ō no Yasumaro compiled it with the help of Hieda no Are, who, it was said, could remember anything after hearing it once.{{fn:kojiki}} The Nihon Shoki followed in 720.{{fn:nihon-shoki}}

The Great Buddha

Emperor Shōmu made Buddhism the protector of the state. At Tōdai-ji, the “great eastern temple”, he had a bronze Buddha cast sixteen metres high, in eight castings over three years. At its eye-opening in 752 an Indian monk, Bodhisena, painted in the eyes before ten thousand monks. The hall around it, rebuilt in 1709, is the one in the print behind this chapter.{{fn:todaiji}}

The monk Jianzhen, Ganjin in Japanese, tried five times between 743 and 748 to sail from China to Japan to teach the rules of ordination. He lost his sight on the way. He landed at last in 753, blind, and founded Tōshōdai-ji in Nara in 759.{{fn:ganjin}}

Ten thousand leaves

The Man’yōshū, “collection of ten thousand leaves”, gathers about 4,500 poems in twenty books, the latest from 759. Emperors, soldiers and farmers’ wives are among its poets. It is written entirely in Chinese characters, many used only for their sound to spell out Japanese words, a system called man’yōgana.{{fn:manyoshu}}

These words introduce thirty-two poems on plum blossoms composed at a party given by Ōtomo no Tabito at Dazaifu in 730. On 1 April 2019 the government announced that the next era would be called Reiwa, from 令 and 和 in this passage. It was the first era name taken from a Japanese classic rather than a Chinese one.{{fn:reiwa,reiwa-dazaifu}}
