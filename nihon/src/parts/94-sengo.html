After the War

A peace constitution, the fastest growth the world had seen, a train at 210 kilometres an hour, and the long years after the boom.

For the first time in its history Japan was occupied by a foreign army. The American-led occupation rewrote its institutions. A new constitution, drafted largely by the occupation’s staff and passed by the Diet, was promulgated on 3 November 1946 and took effect on 3 May 1947. It made the emperor a symbol of the state, and in its ninth article renounced war.{{fn:constitution}} Women voted for the first time on 10 April 1946 and elected thirty-nine of their number to the Diet.{{fn:women-1946}}

The peace treaty was signed at San Francisco on 8 September 1951, and the occupation ended when it came into force on 28 April 1952, except in Okinawa.{{fn:occupation}} Okinawa stayed under American administration for twenty-seven years and returned to Japan on 15 May 1972; the American bases there remain.{{fn:okinawa-1972}}

The miracle

Between 1955 and 1973 the economy grew by about nine percent a year on average, and in 1968 Japan’s national product passed West Germany’s to become the second largest in the world.{{fn:growth}} On 1 October 1964, nine days before Tokyo opened the Olympic Games, the Tōkaidō Shinkansen began running between Tokyo and Shin-Osaka. Its trains, the 0 series, reached 210 kilometres an hour and cut the journey to four hours. It was the first railway in the world built for regular service above 200 kilometres an hour; the print behind this chapter shows one crossing the Fuji River.{{fn:shinkansen}}

The bubble and after

Land and share prices rose through the 1980s until, on 29 December 1989, the Nikkei stock index closed at 38,915.87. Then the bubble burst. The index did not pass that figure again until 22 February 2024.{{fn:nikkei}} The years between are called Japan’s lost decades.

The ground itself moved. The Great Hanshin earthquake of 17 January 1995 killed 6,434 people in and around Kobe, by the official count.{{fn:hanshin}} On 11 March 2011 an earthquake of magnitude 9.1 off the northeast coast raised a tsunami that left more than 18,000 people dead or missing and caused meltdowns in three reactors at the Fukushima Daiichi nuclear power plant.{{fn:tohoku}}

Japan’s population peaked at about 128 million around 2008 and has fallen every year since; the census of 2025 counted about 123 million.{{fn:population,census-2025}}

Reiwa

On 30 April 2019 Emperor Akihito stepped down, the first emperor to abdicate since Kōkaku in 1817. His son Naruhito succeeded him the next day, and the era of Reiwa began.{{fn:abdication,reiwa}}

This year, 2026, is the year of the fire horse, 丙午 hinoe-uma, which comes once in sixty years. A superstition held that girls born in such a year would grow up headstrong and hard to marry, and in 1966, the last fire horse year, births in Japan fell by a quarter. In 2026 the superstition seems to have lost its hold: births in January were slightly up on the year before.{{fn:hinoeuma,hinoeuma-2026}}
