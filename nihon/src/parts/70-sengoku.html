The Warring States

Guns and missionaries from Portugal, three unifiers, and the castles they built.

For a century after the Ōnin War, local lords, the daimyō, fought one another for land. Into this world, in 1543, a storm drove a Chinese junk carrying three Portuguese adventurers onto the island of Tanegashima, south of Kyushu. They were the first Europeans known to set foot in Japan. The island’s lord bought two of their matchlock guns and set a swordsmith to copy them; Japanese smiths were soon making their own, and the gun became known as a tanegashima.{{fn:firearms,portugal}} In 1549 the Jesuit Francis Xavier landed at Kagoshima and began the Christian mission to Japan.{{fn:portugal}}

Three unifiers

Three men put the country back together. The first, Oda Nobunaga, a minor lord from Owari, made his name in 1560 at Okehazama, where his small force surprised and killed the invading warlord Imagawa Yoshimoto.{{fn:okehazama}} In 1575 at Nagashino his musketeers helped crush the army of the Takeda, and the next year he began Azuchi Castle on Lake Biwa, completed in 1579. In June 1582 one of his own generals, Akechi Mitsuhide, surrounded him at the temple of Honnō-ji in Kyoto, and Nobunaga took his own life inside the temple.{{fn:azuchi}}

The second, Toyotomi Hideyoshi, who had risen from a foot soldier, avenged his lord and finished the conquest. In 1588 he ordered the sword hunt, which took weapons from farmers and fixed the line between those who fought and those who worked the land.{{fn:sword-hunt}} His tea master Sen no Rikyū, who had shaped the tea ceremony into an art of simplicity, fell out of favour and was ordered to kill himself in 1591.{{fn:rikyu}} In 1597 twenty-six Christians, six foreign missionaries and twenty Japanese, three of them boys, were crucified at Nagasaki.{{fn:martyrs}}

Hideyoshi also invaded Korea, twice, in 1592 and 1597. His armies took Seoul and Pyongyang, but the Korean navy under Admiral Yi Sun-sin, a Ming army and Korean irregulars stopped them, and the campaign ended with his death in 1598.{{fn:imjin}}

Sekigahara

On 21 October 1600, at Sekigahara, the eastern army of the third, Tokugawa Ieyasu, met the western army of Hideyoshi’s loyalists. The fight was even until midday, when a western commander on the hills above the field, won over in advance, turned on his own side. Three years later Ieyasu was named shogun.{{fn:sekigahara}}

The print behind this chapter shows a castle in the style of the one that was completed at Himeji in 1609, on the site of an older fortress granted to Ikeda Terumasa after Sekigahara. Its white walls and stacked roofs survive intact, and in 1993 it became the first Japanese castle on the World Heritage List.{{fn:himeji}}
