# The Ogura Hyakunin Isshu, translated for this site.
# Format: "§N | poet as usually known in English", then the translation,
# then an optional line beginning "note:".

§1 | Emperor Tenji
The autumn rice fields: in the watchman's hut
the rush thatch is so coarse
my sleeves are wet with dew, and wet again.

§2 | Empress Jitō
Spring has gone, and summer, it seems, has come:
they say white robes are spread to dry
on Heavenly Mount Kagu.

§3 | Kakinomoto no Hitomaro
Long as the trailing tail of the mountain pheasant
is this long, long night.
Must I sleep through it alone?

§4 | Yamabe no Akahito
I come out onto the shore at Tago and look up:
on the high peak of Fuji, white as cloth,
the snow is falling.

§5 | Sarumaru no Taifu
Deep in the mountains, treading through fallen maple leaves,
a stag cries out:
when I hear that voice, autumn is sad.

§6 | Ōtomo no Yakamochi
When I see the frost lie white
on the bridge the magpies spread across the sky,
I know the night has grown late.
note: In the Chinese legend the Weaver and the Herdsman, two stars, meet once a year on a bridge of magpies' wings.

§7 | Abe no Nakamaro
I gaze far out across the plain of heaven:
is that the moon that rose
over Mount Mikasa at Kasuga?
note: Written in China, where the poet had lived for decades and where he died.

§8 | Kisen Hōshi
My hut lies southeast of the capital,
and here I live, just so.
People say I fled the world's gloom to Uji mountain.
note: Uji sounds like ushi, "gloomy".

§9 | Ono no Komachi
The color of the flowers has faded, all in vain,
while I gazed out at the long rains
and grew old in the world.
note: Nagame means both "long rains" and "gazing in thought"; furu both "to fall" and "to grow old".

§10 | Semimaru
So this is the place where those who go and those who return,
friends and strangers,
part and meet: the barrier of Meeting Hill.

§11 | Ono no Takamura
Tell them, fishing boats:
I have rowed out onto the wide sea,
bound past its eighty islands.
note: Written as he set out for exile on the Oki Islands.

§12 | Sōjō Henjō
Winds of heaven, blow shut
the paths through the clouds,
and keep these dancing maidens with us a little longer.

§13 | Emperor Yōzei
From the peak of Mount Tsukuba
the Minano River falls:
so my love has gathered into a deep pool.

§14 | Minamoto no Tōru
Like the tangled patterns of Shinobu cloth from Michinoku,
my heart has fallen into disorder.
For whose sake? Not my own.

§15 | Emperor Kōkō
For you I went out into the spring fields
to gather young greens,
and on my sleeves the snow kept falling.

§16 | Ariwara no Yukihira
I leave you for Inaba,
where pines grow on the mountain peaks;
but if I hear you pine for me, I will come straight back.
note: Matsu means both "pine" and "to wait".

§17 | Ariwara no Narihira
Unheard of even in the age of the raging gods:
the Tatsuta River
tie-dyeing its waters in Chinese crimson.
note: The dye is the maple leaves floating on the river.

§18 | Fujiwara no Toshiyuki
Waves draw near the shore at Suminoe;
yet even at night, on the paths of dreams,
why do you shun the eyes of others?

§19 | Ise
Would you have me go through this life
without meeting you, even for a moment
short as the joints of the reeds in Naniwa Bay?

§20 | Prince Motoyoshi
Wretched as I am, it is all the same now:
like the channel markers of Naniwa,
I will spend myself to meet you.
note: Miotsukushi, "channel marker", also means "to give up one's life".

§21 | Sosei Hōshi
Only because you said you would come soon,
I waited through the long autumn nights
until the moon of dawn came out.

§22 | Fun'ya no Yasuhide
As soon as it blows, the autumn grass and trees wither;
no wonder the wind off the mountain
is written "storm".
note: The character for storm, 嵐, is made of mountain, 山, over wind, 風.

§23 | Ōe no Chisato
When I look at the moon
I am sad in a thousand ways,
though autumn has not come to me alone.

§24 | Sugawara no Michizane
On this journey I could bring no offering.
At Offering Mountain, let this brocade of maple leaves
be for the gods, as they please.

§25 | Fujiwara no Sadakata
If you are true to your name,
sleeping vine of Meeting Hill,
show me a way to draw her to me unseen.

§26 | Fujiwara no Tadahira
Maple leaves on the peak of Mount Ogura,
if you have hearts,
wait for one more royal visit.

§27 | Fujiwara no Kanesuke
Welling up, the Izumi River divides the Mika plain.
When did I ever see her,
that I should long for her so?

§28 | Minamoto no Muneyuki
In a mountain village it is winter that is loneliest,
when I think how both visitors and grasses
have withered away.

§29 | Ōshikōchi no Mitsune
If I pick one, I must pick by guesswork:
the first frost has settled
and confused itself with the white chrysanthemums.

§30 | Mibu no Tadamine
Since we parted under the cold, indifferent moon of dawn,
nothing has been so bitter to me
as daybreak.

§31 | Sakanoue no Korenori
At first light, the white snow fallen on the village of Yoshino
is so bright
I took it for the moon at dawn.

§32 | Harumichi no Tsuraki
The weir the wind has thrown
across the mountain stream
is made of maple leaves that cannot flow away.

§33 | Ki no Tomonori
On a spring day of calm and steady light,
why do the blossoms fall
with such unquiet hearts?

§34 | Fujiwara no Okikaze
Whom shall I have for a friend?
Even the old pines of Takasago
were not my companions long ago.

§35 | Ki no Tsurayuki
As for you, I cannot know your heart;
but in this old place the blossoms
have the scent they always had.

§36 | Kiyohara no Fukayabu
A summer night: it was still evening, and already dawn.
Where in the clouds
has the moon found lodging?

§37 | Fun'ya no Asayasu
On the autumn moor, when the wind blows over the white dew,
pearls that no thread holds
scatter everywhere.

§38 | Ukon
I do not think of myself, forgotten;
but I fear for the life of the man
who swore before the gods to love me.

§39 | Minamoto no Hitoshi
Low reeds, bamboo grass on the little moor:
I hide my love, but it overflows.
Why do I long for you so?

§40 | Taira no Kanemori
I hid it, but my love has shown in my face,
until people ask me
whether something troubles me.

§41 | Mibu no Tadami
Already the rumor is out
that I am in love,
though I had only just begun to love in secret.

§42 | Kiyohara no Motosuke
We promised, didn't we, wringing out
each other's tear-soaked sleeves:
no wave would ever cross the pine hill of Sue.

§43 | Fujiwara no Atsutada
Compared with my heart
since we met,
before, I knew nothing of longing.

§44 | Fujiwara no Asatada
If we had never met at all,
I would not now resent
you, or myself.

§45 | Fujiwara no Koretada
I can think of no one
who would say they pity me,
and so I must waste away for nothing.

§46 | Sone no Yoshitada
Like a boatman crossing the strait of Yura
who has lost his oar,
I drift on love's way, not knowing where.

§47 | Egyō Hōshi
The lonely house is overgrown with creepers;
no one comes to see it,
but autumn has come.

§48 | Minamoto no Shigeyuki
Like the waves the wind drives against a rock,
I alone break to pieces:
these are days of longing.

§49 | Ōnakatomi no Yoshinobu
Like the fires the palace guards light at the gates,
my love burns at night
and dies down by day.

§50 | Fujiwara no Yoshitaka
For your sake I would not have minded losing my life;
now that we have met,
I wish it could be long.

§51 | Fujiwara no Sanekata
I cannot even tell you that I love you.
You cannot know how it burns,
like the moxa of Mount Ibuki.

§52 | Fujiwara no Michinobu
I know that dawn will turn to dusk again
and bring you back,
and still I hate the first light.

§53 | The mother of Fujiwara no Michitsuna
Do you know how long a night can be,
lying alone and grieving
until the dawn?

§54 | The mother of Fujiwara no Korechika
"I will never forget," you say.
Since forever is hard to trust,
let my life end today.

§55 | Fujiwara no Kintō
The sound of the waterfall
stopped long ago,
but its name flows on and is heard still.

§56 | Izumi Shikibu
Soon I will be gone from this world.
To take with me beyond it,
let me see you once more.

§57 | Murasaki Shikibu
We met again, and before I could be sure it was you,
you were gone,
like the midnight moon behind the clouds.

§58 | Daini no Sanmi
When the wind blows across the bamboo grass of Ina
below Mount Arima, it rustles: yes, yes.
Could I forget you?

§59 | Akazome Emon
I should have gone to sleep without waiting.
Instead, as the night wore on,
I watched the moon until it set.

§60 | Koshikibu no Naishi
The road over Mount Ōe through Ikuno is long:
I have not set foot on the Bridge of Heaven,
nor seen a letter from there.
note: Fumi means both "to step" and "a letter". The poet's mother, Izumi Shikibu, was then in Tango, and the court teased the daughter that her mother wrote her poems for her.

§61 | Ise no Taifu
The eightfold cherries
of the old capital of Nara
bloom today in the ninefold palace.

§62 | Sei Shōnagon
In the dead of night you may fool the gatekeeper
with a false cock-crow,
but the barrier of Meeting Hill will never let you through.
note: A Chinese story tells of a man who escaped through a border gate at night by imitating a rooster.

§63 | Fujiwara no Michimasa
Now I only want a way to tell you,
myself and not through another,
that I must give you up.

§64 | Fujiwara no Sadayori
At first light, as the river mist at Uji breaks,
the stakes of the fish weirs appear
one by one across the shallows.

§65 | Sagami
My sleeves, never dry from bitter tears, are bad enough;
it is my name, rotting away in love,
that I regret.

§66 | Gyōson
Let us feel for each other, mountain cherry:
besides your flowers
no one here knows me.

§67 | Suō no Naishi
For an arm as a pillow, as brief as a spring night's dream,
how I would hate the rumor it would raise,
and all for nothing.

§68 | Emperor Sanjō
If against my will I live on
in this sorrowful world,
how I shall miss this midnight moon.

§69 | Nōin Hōshi
Blown by the storm from Mount Mimuro,
the maple leaves become
brocade on the Tatsuta River.

§70 | Ryōzen Hōshi
Lonely, I step out of my hut
and look around: everywhere
the same autumn dusk.

§71 | Minamoto no Tsunenobu
At evening the rice leaves in the fields by the gate rustle,
and the autumn wind blows
through the reed-thatched hut.

§72 | Kii
I've heard of the fickle waves on Takashi beach:
I won't let them near,
or my sleeves will be wet with tears.

§73 | Ōe no Masafusa
The cherries on the high peaks have bloomed.
Mists of the near hills,
do not rise and hide them.

§74 | Minamoto no Toshiyori
I prayed at Hatsuse that her cold heart might soften,
not that it should grow harsher
like the wind that blows down the mountain.

§75 | Fujiwara no Mototoshi
I lived on your promise, dew on the moxa leaves,
and now, alas,
this autumn too is passing.

§76 | Fujiwara no Tadamichi
Rowing out on the wide sea, I look:
far off, the white waves
blur into the clouds.

§77 | Emperor Sutoku
The rapids split on a rock,
but downstream the waters join again:
so we too, parted now, will meet in the end.

§78 | Minamoto no Kanemasa
Keeper of the barrier at Suma,
how many nights have you woken
to the plovers crying on their way to Awaji?

§79 | Fujiwara no Akisuke
Through a gap in the clouds
trailing on the autumn wind,
how clear the moonlight streams.

§80 | Taikenmon'in no Horikawa
I cannot know whether your heart will last.
This morning my black hair is tangled,
and so are my thoughts.

§81 | Fujiwara no Sanesada
I look toward the place
where the cuckoo sang:
only the moon of dawn is left.

§82 | Dōin Hōshi
In my misery I still live on;
it is my tears
that cannot bear the sorrow.

§83 | Fujiwara no Shunzei
There is no way out of this world:
even deep in the mountains where I came to hide,
I hear a stag crying.

§84 | Fujiwara no Kiyosuke
If I live on, I may look back fondly on these days too:
the times I thought were hard
are the ones I miss now.

§85 | Shun'e Hōshi
All through these nights of longing, dawn will not come,
and even the chinks of my bedroom door
are cruel and let in no light.

§86 | Saigyō Hōshi
Is it the moon that tells me to grieve,
that fills me with longing?
My tears pretend it is.

§87 | Jakuren Hōshi
The drops of a passing shower not yet dry on the cypress leaves,
the mist rises:
an autumn evening.

§88 | Kōkamon'in no Bettō
For one night's short sleep, short as a reed's joint in Naniwa Bay,
must I give myself
wholly to love?

§89 | Princess Shikishi
Thread of my life, if you must break, then break:
if I live on, my strength
to hide this love will fail.

§90 | Inpumon'in no Tayū
If only I could show you:
even the fishermen's sleeves at Ojima, soaked through and through,
keep their color. Mine are dyed with tears.

§91 | Fujiwara no Yoshitsune
Crickets cry in the frosty night.
On a cold straw mat, spreading one sleeve,
must I sleep alone?

§92 | Nijōin no Sanuki
My sleeves are like a rock far out at sea,
unseen even at low tide:
no one knows, but they are never dry.

§93 | Minamoto no Sanetomo
If only the world could always stay like this:
a fisherman's small boat towed along the shore,
how moving it is.

§94 | Fujiwara no Masatsune
The autumn wind comes down from the mountains of Yoshino.
Late at night, in the old village, cold,
the sound of cloth being beaten.

§95 | Jien
Unworthy as I am,
from the timbered mountain where I live
I spread my black monk's sleeves over the people of this sorrowful world.

§96 | Fujiwara no Kintsune
It is not the snow of blossoms
the storm sweeps into the garden that is falling:
it is I who am growing old.
note: Furu means both "to fall" and "to grow old".

§97 | Fujiwara no Teika
Waiting for one who does not come,
in the evening calm at Matsuho Bay
I burn like the seaweed they burn for salt.
note: Teika compiled this anthology.

§98 | Fujiwara no Ietaka
The wind rustles the oaks by the brook of Nara at dusk,
like autumn: only the purification rite
says it is summer still.

§99 | Emperor Go-Toba
I cherish some people and resent others;
it is because I brood on this bitter world
that I am so troubled.

§100 | Emperor Juntoku
The palace of a hundred stones: remembrance fern
grows on its old eaves,
and still the past is more than I can long for.
