道

The Way

A word for a road that came to name the source of everything.

A road that speaks

The character 道 dào sets the sign for walking, 辶, beside 首, a head: someone going along a road, or leading the way.{{fn:dao-char}} In Chinese it still means a road, a path, a method, and also to say. The first line of the Daodejing uses both senses at once. The Way that can be way-ed, walked or put into words, is not the constant Way.

Every school had a way

In the centuries before the first empire, every school of thought claimed to know the dao. For Confucius and his followers it was the way of the ancient sage kings: ritual, duty and good government. The Daodejing reaches further back. Its Way comes before heaven and earth, cannot be named, and pays no attention to human purposes.

Straw dogs were ritual effigies. The Zhuangzi describes how they were kept in boxes and wrapped in embroidered cloth before a sacrifice, then trampled by passers-by and burned for kindling once it was over.{{fn:zz}} Heaven and earth are not cruel. They simply do not hold on.

Five ways into the Way

生 Source
The Way gives them life, virtue nourishes them. Everything comes from it and is sustained by it. 51
無名 Nameless
The Way is ever nameless. Words carve things out of the whole; the whole itself has no name. 32
沖 Empty
The Way is an empty vessel; use it, and it is never filled. 4
自然 So of itself
The Way follows what is so of itself. It has no model and no purpose outside itself. 25
反 Returning
Returning is the motion of the Way. What goes out comes back, and opposites turn into each other. 40

Taoism, Daoism, and the two words behind them

Tao and Dao are the same word, 道, spelled in two systems of romanization: the older Wade–Giles and pinyin, the system used in China today. This site uses pinyin for Chinese names and terms (Dao, Laozi, Zhuangzi) and keeps the tradition’s most familiar English name, Taoism.

English uses one word where Chinese sources use two. Daojia 道家, the house or lineage of the Way, began as a label in a survey of the schools of thought by the Han historian Sima Tan, who died in 110 BCE.{{fn:sima-tan}} Daojiao 道教, the teaching of the Way, names the organized religion with its priests, rites and scriptures. For a long time Western scholars split these into a pure early philosophy and a later, corrupted religion. Most specialists now reject the split. The Taoist canon never made it, and Taoists recited, practiced and commented on the old books for two thousand years.{{fn:sep-religious,kirkland}}
