修煉

Cultivation

Breath, stillness, the alchemist’s furnace and the sacred mountains.

The character for an immortal, 仙 xiān, sets a person, 亻, beside a mountain, 山. Taoist practice aims at health and long life, and beyond them at transformation: a body and mind refined until they return to the Way.

Guiding and pulling

When archaeologists opened tomb 3 at Mawangdui in 1973 they found, beside the silk Laozi, a painted chart of 44 figures doing exercises of daoyin, guiding and pulling: stretching, bending and breathing to move qi through the body. It is the ancestor of today’s qigong.{{fn:daoyin}} The Zhuangzi already knew the type, and was not wholly impressed:

Taijiquan, the slow, circling exercise known across the world as tai chi, joined UNESCO’s list of intangible heritage in 2020. UNESCO traces it to the mid-seventeenth century in Wenxian county, Henan.{{fn:taijiquan}} A later legend credits the Wudang immortal Zhang Sanfeng with inventing it.{{fn:zhang-sanfeng}}

The furnace

Alchemists looked for immortality in the crucible. Ge Hong (283–343) gave recipes for an elixir of gold and cinnabar in his Baopuzi.{{fn:ge-hong}} Such elixirs were made of minerals like cinnabar, mercury sulfide, and they could kill; the emperors of the ninth century learned this the hard way.{{fn:elixir}} The same experiments led to one of history’s great accidents. A Taoist text of the ninth or tenth century warns that some alchemists who heated sulfur, realgar and saltpeter with honey burned their hands and faces, and even their houses: an early description of gunpowder. The first formula written down for use in war appears in the military manual Wujing zongyao of 1044.{{fn:zmy,gunpowder}}

In time the furnace moved inside the body. Inner alchemy, neidan, treats the body as the laboratory and three of its own substances, essence (精 jīng), breath (氣 qì) and spirit (神 shén), as the ingredients. Essence is refined into breath, breath into spirit, and spirit returned to emptiness.{{fn:neidan}} Zhang Boduan’s Awakening to Reality, 81 poems written in 1075, became its classic.{{fn:wuzhen}} In 1929 Richard Wilhelm and Carl Jung introduced a later inner-alchemy manual, The Secret of the Golden Flower, to Western readers.{{fn:golden-flower}}

Mountains

Taoists found paradise inside the landscape. Tang masters mapped ten great and thirty-six lesser grotto-heavens, hollow worlds inside mountains where immortals live, and seventy-two blessed lands.{{fn:grotto}} Three Taoist mountains are now World Heritage sites: Wudang, rebuilt as an imperial complex under the Ming emperor Zhu Di (inscribed 1994); Qingcheng in Sichuan, counted among the birthplaces of the religion (2000); and Sanqingshan, whose three peaks are named for the Three Pure Ones (2008).{{fn:wudang,qingcheng,sanqing}}
