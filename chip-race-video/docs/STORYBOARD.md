# Silicon Shield: storyboard (full song, 128 BPM, bar = 1.875 s)

Each row is one plate, i.e. one scene module in `app/src/scenes/<id>.ts`. Windows are in bars and seconds (from src/song.py). Look up lyric times through the `Lyrics` API by content, never hard-code them. **Idiom** is the plate's unique visual instrument; never reuse another plate's idiom. **Out** is the mandatory handoff into the next plate.

Through-lines: the amber **beam**, the **TPP meter** (1,200 → 4,800 threshold → 19,200 → ∞ → RESTRICTED), **sand**, the **wafer**. See STYLE_BIBLE.md.

## Act I: the machine (0–26 s)

| id | bars / s | lyric | idiom and key moves | out |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `sand` | 0–4 / 0–7.5 | (intro) | **Powers-of-ten microscope dive.** It opens on black. A single grain of quartz sand (raymarched, engraved hatching, rim light) sits on an ink field with a live scale ruler (`1 mm`). A continuous log-zoom runs on the beat grid through grain, then crystal facets, then a silicon lattice (diamond-cubic, hairline bonds) to `0.5 nm`. The ruler's digits roll; mono footnotes are deadpan ("SiO₂ · 99.9999999% after refining"). Bells play the hook motif. On bar 2 the TITLE assembles from lattice atoms snapping to glyph points (`textPoints`), then dissolves on bar 3. The last beat: one lattice atom ignites amber. **Must be the strongest opening.** | The ignited atom **is** the tin droplet: match cut, same screen position. |
| `tin` | 4–6 / 7.5–11.25 | Fifty thousand tin drops every second | **Edgerton stroboscope photography.** A black chamber; the droplet stream is frozen in strobe flashes on every 16th. Each flash leaves a fading multiple-exposure ghost (a stacked exposure column). Below it, a timing diagram (pulse train, mono) scrolls in sync. The lyric is a high-speed camera slate: FIFTY THOUSAND as a slate counter clacking up per syllable, "tin drops every second" chalked by the beam. The `50,000 Hz` footnote. Camera: locked, then a punch on each downbeat. | A flash freezes on one droplet that pancakes (pre-pulse), becoming `laser`'s target. |
| `laser` | 6–8 / 11.25–15 | Shot by a laser, hotter than the sun | **Thermography.** A false-colour heat image in the single-hue amber ramp, with isotherm contour lines breathing outward and a temperature scale bar at the right edge. On "laser" the main pulse makes the frame go white-hot for 2 frames, then a plasma ball expands with animated isotherms. "hotter than the sun": the scale bar **extends off the top of the frame**, and the camera tilts up the bar past a tiny `☉ 5,500 °C` tick to `220,000 °C · ×40`. SUN is set tiny next to the sun tick. | Isotherm rings become the **mirrors** of `machine` (match: concentric rings). |
| `machine` | 8–10 / 15–18.75 | One Dutch machine, and the whole world's waiting | **Exploded axonometric cutaway** (technical-manual engraving, hidden-line hairlines, hatched sections). "and the whole world's waiting": a crane-out through the hall roof to a **dot-matrix world map** at night; every lit dot sends a queue line to one point (Veldhoven), with an ORDER BACKLOG counter. | The converging queue lines bundle into **one cable**, which becomes the crane's cable in `tons`. |
| `tons` | 10–12 / 18.75–22.5 | Hundred eighty tons, and everybody wants one | **Freight crane, top-down.** A container yard seen from directly above (orthographic, engraved). 40 containers drop into place on 16ths and spell **180 t**; a camera shake on "tons". The flaps flip with motion blur and sound. | The board's flap edges become **horizontal lines of newsprint** (`line`). |
| `line` | 12–13.75 / 22.5–25.78 | Then Washington drew a line in the sand | **Newspaper halftone.** Bone paper: a broadsheet front page made of halftone dots (the world map as halftone), with a mono dateline. A riser plays and the camera pushes in until halftone dots fill the frame, and the dots **are sand**. The tape stop slows the whole picture to a halt. | Gap (25.78–26.25): black, the a cappella "sand" in serif dissolves into grains. White flash into the drop. |

## Sync and QA requirements (every plate)
- Big changes land on beats: downbeat cuts, kick punches, word slams at `word.start`. Use `data/events.json` (kick, snare, impact, riser, gap, tapestop, stutter, sfx) when present.
- Each plate's owner renders a contact sheet plus dense stills around its key moves, and runs the motion QA (no unintended static stretches longer than 1.2 s).
- Handoffs: the last 0.25 s of each plate must set up the first frame of the next. Coordinate through the "out" column; the lead verifies cut pairs with the timeline `sheet --cuts`.
