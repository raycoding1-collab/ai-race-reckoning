Interlude · The grammar of anime

How anime makes space, time and feeling

By the late 1980s Japanese animation had a visual language of its own. Some of it came from economy, some from individual artists, and some from older habits of picture-making. Here are its main working parts, starting with the one scholars argue about most: how anime makes depth.

Layers that slide

Anime usually makes depth by sliding flat layers past each other at different speeds: near things rush, far things drift, and the viewer never travels into the picture. The film theorist Thomas Lamarre called this “animetism”.

A camera that moves in

Disney's multiplane camera, introduced in 1937, did the opposite. It spaced painted layers vertically and moved the camera down through them, so the audience seemed to travel into depth. Lamarre called that “cinematism”.

Why it matters

Sliding layers are cheap, and they produce a particular feeling: watching a world go by, as from a train. It is one reason trains, skies and long lateral journeys recur so often in anime.

間

Ma, the pause

Moments where nothing happens on purpose: a sigh, a long look, rain on an umbrella. They let tension spread out into atmosphere.

Totoro's bus stop · Spirited Away's train ride · Mushishi

ハーモニー

The harmony frame

At a climax the character is repainted in the textured style of the background and frozen, like an illustration. A still that carries more feeling than motion could.

Osamu Dezaki: Tomorrow's Joe · The Rose of Versailles · Aim for the Ace!

板野サーカス

The Itano Circus

Dozens of missiles, each on its own corkscrew of smoke, shot as if the camera were flying among them. Ichirō Itano's signature, imitated in mecha action ever since.

Macross (1982) · Macross Plus · Gunbuster

金田エフェクト

Kanada effects

Angular flames, explosions shaped like shards, poses snapped hard into extreme perspective. Yoshinori Kanada's style defined the look of late-1970s and 1980s action.

Galaxy Express 999 · Harmagedon · the young Hideaki Anno's explosions

作画

Sakuga

Individual key animators are credited and followed by fans, who clip and share their standout sequences. Animation is treated as authorship at the level of the single shot.

Fan archives such as Sakugabooru · Yutaka Nakamura's fight scenes

美術

Background art

Painted backgrounds carry mood and place. Kazuo Oga's forests for Ghibli and the photographic skies of Makoto Shinkai turned scenery into a lead character.

My Neighbor Totoro · Only Yesterday · Your Name

撮影

Compositing

Once the photographing of cels, now the digital department that layers everything and adds light, glow, blur and atmosphere. Much of what makes modern anime look “expensive” happens here.

ufotable's water and fire in Demon Slayer

漫符

Emotion symbols

A sweat drop, a throbbing vein, a sudden shift to tiny “super-deformed” proportions. A shorthand borrowed from manga that turns inner states into visible marks.

Almost every comedy · Bocchi the Rock! pushes it into abstraction

変身

Transformation

The hero changes into a new self in a sequence reused every episode. Cheap to repeat, and repetition made it a ritual of identity that viewers know by heart.

Cutie Honey · Sailor Moon · Madoka Magica turns it dark

モチーフ

Eight images anime keeps returning to

Trains 電車

The space between places and between stages of life. Journeys on rails mark passage, longing and farewell.

Skies 空

Weather as emotion. Clouds, light and dusk say what characters can't, nowhere more than in Shinkai's films.

Food 食

Care made visible. A shared meal is how anime shows that someone is safe, loved or home.

Rain 雨

Waiting, shelter and the permission to stop. Rain creates the pause in which people finally talk.

Flight 飛行

Freedom and its price. Miyazaki's aircraft are beautiful machines that are also weapons.

Ruins 廃墟

After the end. Postwar memory and the bomb echo through drowned cities, overgrown castles and wrecked Tokyos.

Cherry blossom 桜

[[mononoaware]]: beauty that matters because it ends. Spring, graduation and parting all at once.

The back view 背中

A character seen from behind, facing something larger. Resolve and loneliness without a word of dialogue.
