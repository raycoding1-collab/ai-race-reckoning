How this history works

Ten reels, four questions

Anime is not a genre. It is a medium and an industry that has held every kind of story, from boxing melodrama to Shinto ecology to cyberpunk philosophy. Its history is told here in ten reels, from medieval picture scrolls to the global box office of 2025. Each reel asks the same four questions, so you can compare one era with the next.

Featured works are laid out like the rows of an 絵コンテ e-konte, the storyboard sheet every anime is planned on. Each has a cut number, a picture, its content and its time. Dotted words such as [[sakuga]] open a short definition.

背景Context

The world around it

Money, technology, war, disaster, television schedules, home video, streaming. Many of anime's signature looks began as answers to a constraint.

意図Intent

What the artists reached for

What the makers were trying to say, in their own words where a reliable record exists, and clearly labelled as interpretation where it does not.

内面Interiority

How it felt

The inner lives the works show, and what they did to the inner lives of the people watching.

波紋Ripples

What it changed

Impact in Japan and around the world, and one person or group whose life the era touched.

The apocalyptic

Cities levelled and worlds ended: Akira, Evangelion, Attack on Titan.

The festival

Carnival, chaos and comic release: Lupin III, Urusei Yatsura, Gurren Lagann.

The elegiac

Mourning for what is passing: Grave of the Fireflies, 5 Centimeters per Second, Frieren.

The scholar Susan J. Napier argued that anime keeps returning to three modes: the apocalyptic, the festival and the elegiac. The labels are hers. The examples include later works she did not discuss.
