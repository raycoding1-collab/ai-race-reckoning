Reel 05

1984 — 1989

The Golden Age

Bubble-economy money, home video and a generation of directors at full strength produced the densest run of masterpieces anime has known, culminating in a single year: 1988.

黄金時代

11 March 1984

Nausicaä of the Valley of the Wind opens. Its success gives Hayao Miyazaki and Isao Takahata a studio of their own.

15 June 1985

Studio Ghibli is founded, backed by the publisher Tokuma Shoten. Two days earlier, an American distributor has released Nausicaä with about 23 minutes cut.

16 April 1988

My Neighbor Totoro and Grave of the Fireflies open together as a double bill: a gentle fantasy and one of the saddest films ever made.

16 July 1988

Akira opens: 160,000 cels, 327 colours, a budget of about ¥1.1 billion.

29 December 1989

The Nikkei stock index hits its all-time peak. The bubble is about to burst.

背景

The world around it

In the second half of the 1980s Japan's asset bubble made money easy. Publishers, toy makers, broadcasters, advertising agencies and trading companies all wanted a piece of media, and animation was a place to put it. A new model of financing spread: the [[seisakuiinkai|production committee]], in which several companies share a film's cost and rights. Akira was made by one, the Akira Committee, whose members included the publisher Kodansha, the toy maker Bandai, the studio Toho, the agency Hakuhodo and the trading house Sumitomo.

Home video added a second market. After Dallos in 1983, [[ova|OVAs]] multiplied: the cyberpunk Bubblegum Crisis (1987), the realist police-robot drama Patlabor (1988), Hideaki Anno's directing debut Gunbuster (1988), and the space opera Legend of the Galactic Heroes, which ran to 110 episodes between 1988 and 1997. Fans would pay for stories too adult, too long or too strange for television. Television itself was enjoying a golden age of manga adaptations from Weekly Shōnen Jump: Fist of the North Star (1984), Dragon Ball (1986), Saint Seiya (1986) and City Hunter (1987).

Above all, it was an age of directors. Hayao Miyazaki had begun Nausicaä as a manga in Animage in 1982, partly because investors would not fund an original film; the film version, released in 1984, was a hit, and in 1985 he, Isao Takahata and producer Toshio Suzuki founded Studio Ghibli. Mamoru Oshii made the dream-logic comedy Urusei Yatsura 2: Beautiful Dreamer (1984) and the near-wordless Angel's Egg (1985). A team of twenty-somethings at the new studio Gainax made Royal Space Force: The Wings of Honnêamise (1987), funded by Bandai with a total cost, including advertising, of about ¥800 million. Yoshiyuki Tomino closed the first Gundam saga with Char's Counterattack (1988).

Then came 1988. On 16 April Ghibli released My Neighbor Totoro and Grave of the Fireflies as a double bill, the second adapted from Akiyuki Nosaka's semi-autobiographical 1967 story of two children in the firebombing of Kobe. On 16 July Katsuhiro Ōtomo's Akira opened: around 160,000 cels and 327 colours, 50 of them mixed for the film; dialogue recorded before animation so that lips could match it; a Tokyo of 2019, rebuilt after a third world war, rendered in more detail than any animated city before.

The decade ended badly. Emperor Hirohito died on 7 January 1989 and Osamu Tezuka a month later. In July the police arrested Tsutomu Miyazaki, who had murdered four young girls. Reporters found 5,763 videotapes in his room, among them anime and horror films, and named him “the otaku murderer”. A moral panic followed, and for years [[otaku]] meant something sinister. Six days after the arrest, Ghibli released Kiki's Delivery Service, the studio's first big box-office hit. On the last trading day of the year the Nikkei index peaked. Within months the bubble had begun to burst.

意図・内面

What they reached for, and how it felt

Intent. Miyazaki drew Nausicaä's poisoned forest partly from the mercury poisoning of Minamata Bay, and from a Heian-period tale of a princess who loved insects. His heroine wins by understanding what everyone else wants to destroy, and the film has an environmental crisis but no simple villain. With Totoro he set out to make a film in Japan, about the countryside of the 1950s, so that Japanese children could find wonder near home. Takahata, surprisingly, rejected the idea that Grave of the Fireflies was an anti-war film. He said he wanted to show a brother and sister who die after cutting themselves off from the people around them, and he aimed it at young viewers of the 1980s. It works powerfully as an anti-war film anyway, and both readings can stand. Ōtomo's Akira is about youth and power: boys from nowhere, a city that keeps destroying and rebuilding itself, and a government playing with a force it does not understand.

Interiority. Each of the 1988 films is built around a child's inner world. Totoro is really about fear: two girls whose mother is in hospital, and a big, silent creature who simply waits with them. Miyazaki's own mother spent years bedridden with spinal tuberculosis. Grave of the Fireflies shows hunger and pride from the inside, down to a four-year-old's tin of fruit drops and a jar of dead fireflies. Akira turns adolescent rage into body horror: Tetsuo, always the one rescued by his friend Kaneda, gains godlike power and his body swells out of control. Kiki is about a young woman alone in a new city who suddenly loses her ability to fly, the clearest picture of burnout and creative block Ghibli ever made.

1989

波紋

Ripples

In Japan

  • Ghibli built a brand on directors rather than characters, and Totoro became its face.
  • The production committee became the standard way to finance anime, spreading risk and splitting rights, often leaving studios with little ownership.
  • The 1989 murder case attached a stigma to fans that lasted a decade.

Around the world

Akira opened in American art-house cinemas on Christmas Day 1989 and became a cult hit on VHS; in Britain its 1991 video release launched the company that became Manga Entertainment. Robotech and Voltron filled American afternoons, and Saint Seiya, Dragon Ball and Captain Tsubasa swept France, Spain, Italy and Latin America.
