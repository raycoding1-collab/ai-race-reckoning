Reel 08

2011 — 2019

After the Wave

The earthquake and tsunami of March 2011 changed what Japanese stories needed to say. Streaming let Japan and the world watch the same episode on the same night. And in 2019 a fire at Kyoto Animation showed how much this art had come to mean to people everywhere.

背景

The world around it

On 11 March 2011 the strongest earthquake ever recorded in Japan sent a tsunami into the coast of Tōhoku, killing or leaving missing more than 18,000 people, and caused meltdowns at the Fukushima Daiichi nuclear plant. In the weeks after, broadcasters practised jishuku, self-restraint. Puella Magi Madoka Magica, a magical-girl series that had turned out to be about despair and sacrifice, had its final two episodes postponed until 21 April. They aired back to back to an audience that had been waiting through a national catastrophe.

The decade's stories kept returning to disaster, memory and the wish to undo the past. Makoto Shinkai said Your Name (2016), in which a comet destroys a lakeside town, came directly from the earthquake. It earned about ¥25 billion in Japan and became, at the time, the highest-grossing anime film worldwide. That autumn also brought Naoko Yamada's A Silent Voice, about a former bully and the deaf girl he tormented, and Sunao Katabuchi's In This Corner of the World, about a young wife's daily life in wartime Kure and Hiroshima. Katabuchi could not raise money for it from the usual investors; 3,374 people funded its pilot through the crowdfunding site Makuake, then a Japanese record.

The masters made their farewells. Miyazaki's The Wind Rises (2013) is a biography of Jiro Horikoshi, designer of the Zero fighter, and an honest look at an artist whose beautiful dream became a weapon. He announced his retirement that September (it did not last). Takahata spent eight years and ¥5.15 billion on The Tale of the Princess Kaguya (2013), drawn in charcoal and watercolour strokes that seem to be still drying. He died in 2018.

The audience went global in real time. Crunchyroll and others began streaming episodes abroad within hours of Japanese broadcast, and Netflix started commissioning anime of its own, such as Masaaki Yuasa's Devilman Crybaby (2018). Attack on Titan (2013–2023) became a worldwide obsession; in 2021 the analytics firm Parrot Analytics named it the most in-demand TV show in the world, the first non-English series to top its list. Light and web novels fed an [[isekai]] boom of stories about being reborn in another world. Web-native animators were recruited onto shows such as One-Punch Man (2015) and Mob Psycho 100 (2016). And in 2019 ufotable's Demon Slayer showed what digital compositing could add to hand-drawn action.

On 18 July 2019 a man walked into Kyoto Animation's Studio 1 carrying petrol and set the building alight. Thirty-six people were killed and more than thirty injured, most of them artists. It was one of the deadliest mass killings in Japan since the Second World War. Donations of about ¥3.3 billion came from within Japan and more than US$2 million from abroad, and the studio passed all of it to victims and their families. The attacker was sentenced to death in January 2024; in 2025 he withdrew his appeal and the sentence became final.

意図・内面

What they reached for, and how it felt

Intent. Shinkai wanted to imagine a world in which the people of a doomed town could be saved, and gave his audience the ending reality had refused. Katabuchi wanted to show that ordinary life went on under the bombs: cooking with weeds, mending clothes, drawing. He spent six years researching wartime Kure, gathering thousands of photographs and checking the weather for every day of the story. Yamada, whose camera lingers on hands and feet, wanted to show how hard it is for people to reach each other. Miyazaki took the epigraph of The Wind Rises from Paul Valéry, “The wind is rising, we must try to live”, and gave his designer's mentor the line that airplanes are beautiful dreams and cursed dreams at once. Takahata wanted to show the joy of being alive on Earth, and the cost of being kept from it. Hajime Isayama built Attack on Titan on walls, and then made his readers question who was really outside them.

Interiority. These are stories about grief and repair. In Your Name two people search for someone they can no longer remember, a feeling of loss without an object that many viewers recognised after 2011. A Silent Voice covers every face around its anxious hero with an X, and lets them fall away one by one as he learns to look at people again. In In This Corner of the World the heroine loses her right hand, her drawing hand, and the film's style changes with her grief. Violet Evergarden (2018), from Kyoto Animation, follows a former child soldier who writes letters for others while trying to understand the last words her commander said to her: “I love you.” Madoka ends with a girl who saves everyone by becoming a principle of the universe, and so can never be seen by her friends again.

The Wind Rises風立ちぬ

Hayao Miyazaki · Studio Ghibli

It says
Beauty and harm can come from the same dream. A gentle engineer designs the most beautiful plane of his age, and it goes to war.
It feels
Restrained and grief-struck: an earthquake that seems to breathe, sound effects made from human voices, a wife dying while her husband works.
It changed
It was controversial in Japan and abroad for its subject, and was received as Miyazaki's most personal reckoning with his own love of machines.

2019

波紋

Ripples

In Japan

  • Anime became a vehicle for working through 3.11, from Your Name to Suzume (2022).
  • Crowdfunding and streaming money opened routes around the production-committee system.
  • The Kyoto Animation fire forced a public reckoning with the safety and conditions of the people who make anime.

Around the world

Simulcasting ended the long wait between Japan and everyone else. Your Name became, at the time, the highest-grossing Japanese film in China. Attack on Titan and Demon Slayer made anime part of mainstream conversation on every continent, and Netflix began commissioning anime for a global audience.

One story

Thirty-six names

After the fire, fans around the world held vigils and raised money. When Kyoto Animation's next film opened that September, its credits listed everyone who had worked on it, including the colleagues who had been killed.
