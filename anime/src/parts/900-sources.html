Sources and further reading

Where this history comes from

Dates, figures and quotations were checked against the sources below. Where accounts disagree, as with the dating of the earliest films, the text says so. Box-office and market figures are in nominal yen and dollars, not adjusted for inflation.

Books

  • Jonathan Clements, Anime: A History (BFI/Palgrave Macmillan, 2013).
  • Susan J. Napier, Anime from Akira to Howl's Moving Castle: Experiencing Contemporary Japanese Animation (Palgrave Macmillan, 2005), the source of the three modes.
  • Susan J. Napier, Miyazakiworld: A Life in Art (Yale University Press, 2018).
  • Thomas Lamarre, The Anime Machine: A Media Theory of Animation (University of Minnesota Press, 2009), on animetism and the multiplanar image.
  • Marc Steinberg, Anime's Media Mix: Franchising Toys and Characters in Japan (University of Minnesota Press, 2012), on Astro Boy and the media mix.
  • Kōichi Iwabuchi, Recentering Globalization: Popular Culture and Japanese Transnationalism (Duke University Press, 2002), on “culturally odourless” exports.
  • Hiroki Azuma, Otaku: Japan's Database Animals (University of Minnesota Press, 2009).
  • Ian Condry, The Soul of Anime: Collaborative Creativity and Japan's Media Success Story (Duke University Press, 2013).
  • Frederik L. Schodt, The Astro Boy Essays (Stone Bridge Press, 2007).
  • Helen McCarthy, The Art of Osamu Tezuka: God of Manga (Abrams ComicArts, 2009).
  • Hayao Miyazaki, Starting Point: 1979–1996 (VIZ Media, 2009) and Turning Point: 1997–2008 (VIZ Media, 2014).
  • Isao Takahata, 十二世紀のアニメーション (Twelfth-Century Animation; Tokuma Shoten, 1999).
  • Andrew Osmond, Satoshi Kon: The Illusionist (Stone Bridge Press, 2009).
  • Jonathan Clements and Helen McCarthy, The Anime Encyclopedia (Stone Bridge Press, 3rd edition, 2015).

Archives and data

  • National Film Archive of Japan, Japanese Animated Film Classics, which launched in 2017 with 64 films from 1917 to 1941, free with English subtitles (Anime News Network, 2017).
  • Association of Japanese Animations, Anime Industry Reports, via Anime News Network (2025) and earlier years' coverage.
  • Kōgyō Tsūshinsha box-office figures, via Anime News Network (April 2026) on the end of Infinity Castle's run in Japan, Famitsu and Nippon.com on its global total, and Oricon (February 2026) on Kokuho.
  • Parrot Analytics, 2021 Global TV Demand Awards.
  • Netflix's anime figures from Anime Expo 2025, reported by Advanced Television.
  • NAFCA working-conditions survey (2024), reported by Cartoon Brew. The Jujutsu Kaisen schedule complaints: Dexerto, 2023.
