The makers

Twenty people who shaped it

Anime is made by hundreds of people per production, most of them uncredited outside Japan. These twenty, directors and also a colourist, an animator and writers, changed what the medium could do. Names are given in Japanese with the family name first.

手塚治虫

Osamu Tezuka

1928–1989

The “god of manga” who brought cinematic storytelling to comics, then built the TV anime industry with Astro Boy.

Astro Boy · Kimba · Phoenix

奥山玲子

Reiko Okuyama

1936–2007

Hired at Toei in 1957 as an in-betweener, she rose to become one of the first women to direct animation in Japan.

Hakujaden · Hols · Natsuzora's model

高畑勲

Isao Takahata

1935–2018

The intellectual of Ghibli: a realist of everyday life who could also make the most formally daring film of his era.

Heidi · Grave of the Fireflies · Kaguya

宮崎駿

Hayao Miyazaki

born 1941

Animator, director and co-founder of Studio Ghibli. Flight, children, ecology and the double nature of machines, across films that have won two Oscars.

Totoro · Spirited Away · The Boy and the Heron

出﨑統

Osamu Dezaki

1943–2011

Made the still frame dramatic: harmony cels, split screens and repeated takes became TV anime's language of intensity.

Tomorrow's Joe · The Rose of Versailles

富野由悠季

Yoshiyuki Tomino

born 1941

Turned the robot show into war literature, with teenage soldiers on both sides and no clean victories.

Mobile Suit Gundam · Char's Counterattack

松本零士

Leiji Matsumoto

1938–2023

Poet of doomed voyages and romantic farewells in space, trains and battleships among the stars.

Space Battleship Yamato · Galaxy Express 999

金田伊功

Yoshinori Kanada

1952–2009

The animator's animator: angular flames, snapping timing and dramatic poses that a generation copied frame by frame.

Galaxy Express 999 · Harmagedon

保田道世

Michiyo Yasuda

1939–2016

Ghibli's colour designer from the start until 2014. “Color has a meaning,” she said, “and it makes the film more easily understood.”

Colour design for nearly every Ghibli film

大友克洋

Katsuhiro Ōtomo

born 1954

A manga artist whose detailed, unglamorous realism, and whose one great film, reset the ambitions of animation worldwide.

Akira · Memories

押井守

Mamoru Oshii

born 1951

Philosopher of dreams, dogs, cities and the self, who made anime into slow, cerebral cinema.

Angel's Egg · Patlabor 2 · Ghost in the Shell

庵野秀明

Hideaki Anno

born 1960

Fan turned animator turned director, who made depression and recovery the subject of a robot franchise.

Gunbuster · Evangelion · Shin Godzilla

今敏

Satoshi Kon

1963–2010

Master of the edit that slips between reality, memory and performance. Four features and one TV series between 1997 and 2006.

Perfect Blue · Millennium Actress · Paprika

幾原邦彦

Kunihiko Ikuhara

born 1964

Theatrical, symbolic, queer: he took shōjo fantasy apart and rebuilt it as avant-garde stagecraft.

Sailor Moon · Revolutionary Girl Utena

渡辺信一郎

Shinichirō Watanabe

born 1965

Directs like a DJ, mixing genres and music. His shows taught the West that anime could be cool.

Cowboy Bebop · Samurai Champloo · Lazarus

湯浅政明

Masaaki Yuasa

born 1965

The freest line in the business: rubbery, joyful, unafraid of looking rough.

Mind Game · Ping Pong · Devilman Crybaby

細田守

Mamoru Hosoda

born 1967

Families, time and the internet, in original films released every few years since 2006.

Summer Wars · Wolf Children · Mirai

新海誠

Makoto Shinkai

born 1973

From a one-man film on a home computer to some of the most successful original anime films ever made. Light, distance, disaster.

Voices of a Distant Star · Your Name · Suzume

岡田麿里

Mari Okada

born 1976

One of anime's most prolific screenwriters, unafraid of raw adolescent emotion, who began directing in 2018.

Anohana · Maquia

山田尚子

Naoko Yamada

born 1984

A director of glances and footsteps who finds drama in how people stand, wait and almost speak.

K-On! · A Silent Voice · The Colors Within

This is a list of people who made anime. Manga artists whose stories it adapted, such as Hiromu Arakawa, Hajime Isayama and Naoki Urasawa, appear in the reels beside the adaptations, Urasawa with Monster (2000–2010) and Pluto (2020–2026).
