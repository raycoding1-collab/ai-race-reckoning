1974 — 1983

Space Battleships and Mobile Suits

The children who grew up with Astro Boy became teenagers and refused to grow out of it. Their devotion created fandom, made “anime” a household word, and pushed creators to treat war, love and loss as seriously as any novel.

The world around it背景

When the fans became makers

The 1973 oil shock ended Japan's years of double-digit growth, and the collapse of Mushi Production scattered its talent across new studios: Sunrise, Madhouse, Nippon Animation, alongside Toei, Tatsunoko and Tokyo Movie Shinsha. Toy companies became the main sponsors of TV animation, and robot shows became toy catalogues. Yet the audience was changing. The first generation raised on TV anime was now in high school and university, and it wanted more.

Space Battleship Yamato (1974) gave it to them. Produced by Yoshinobu Nishizaki with designs by the manga artist Leiji Matsumoto, it raises the wreck of the battleship Yamato, sunk in 1945, and rebuilds it as a starship to fetch a cure for an Earth poisoned by radioactive bombardment. On TV it lost to Heidi. In reruns it found teenagers, and in 1977 a compilation film drew lines around cinemas. Magazines for older fans followed, led by Animage in 1978, and fans began organising: fan clubs, cel collecting, and from 1975 the Comic Market.

Meanwhile Isao Takahata, Hayao Miyazaki and the designer Yōichi Kotabe made Heidi, Girl of the Alps (1974) after a research trip to Switzerland, then a rarity for TV animation. It began a tradition of literary adaptations later branded World Masterpiece Theater, carried by Takahata's 3000 Leagues in Search of Mother (1976) and Anne of Green Gables (1979). Miyazaki directed his first series, Future Boy Conan, for NHK in 1978, and his first feature, The Castle of Cagliostro, in 1979.

1979 was the hinge. Sunrise's Mobile Suit Gundam, directed by Yoshiyuki Tomino, told a war story in which giant robots were mass-produced weapons with model numbers, piloted by frightened teenagers. Its toy sponsor wanted brightly coloured super robots; ratings were poor and the show was cut from 52 episodes to 43. Then Bandai's plastic model kits, launched in 1980, and three compilation films turned it into a phenomenon. That same year brought Rintaro's film of Matsumoto's Galaxy Express 999, the long-running Doraemon, and The Rose of Versailles, from Riyoko Ikeda's shōjo manga about a woman raised as a man to command the Queen's guard.

Fans were becoming makers. Students in Osaka produced short opening films for the 1981 and 1983 Japan science-fiction conventions, DAICON III and IV, that astonished professionals. Several of them, including Hideaki Anno, would found the studio Gainax. In 1983 the columnist Akio Nakamori mocked the new fans as [[otaku]], and the label stuck. In December that year Dallos became the first [[ova|original video animation]], made to be sold straight to fans on tape. And in 1982 Super Dimension Fortress Macross combined transforming fighter jets, a love triangle and a teenage pop idol whose songs end an interstellar war.

What they reached for, and how it felt意図・内面

A pilot who sulks and cries

Intent. Yamato resurrects the symbol of Japan's defeat as a ship that saves the world, and critics have argued ever since whether that is nationalist nostalgia or a story of sacrifice for peace; it can be read both ways. Tomino wanted a war with no simple villains: both sides of Gundam's war have reasons, its heroes do terrible things, and its hope lies in “Newtypes”, people who might come to understand one another without words. Takahata believed daily life was drama enough. In Heidi the events are cheese toasting over a fire, goat milk, weather, and a homesick child in a city flat. Shōji Kawamori and the makers of Macross asked what would happen if culture, not weapons, decided a war.

Interiority. These were the first anime that teenagers felt took them seriously. Amuro Ray is sulky, frightened and resentful, and when he is slapped he cries that not even his father has ever hit him. Many fans recognised themselves in a robot pilot for the first time. In Galaxy Express 999 a boy crosses the galaxy to get a machine body that will let him live forever, and learns that a life which ends is the only kind that means anything. Its last farewell, to the mysterious Maetel, plays as a farewell to boyhood. Heidi's sleepwalking in Frankfurt shows a child's homesickness as an illness, without melodrama. And around these works a community grew whose members found, at conventions and fan markets, that they were not alone.

Space Battleship Yamato宇宙戦艦ヤマト

Yoshinobu Nishizaki, Leiji Matsumoto · 1974 · film 1977

It says
A doomed crew sacrifices everything to bring back a cure for a radioactive Earth, aboard the ghost of the warship that went down in 1945.
It feels
The whole voyage leads to Captain Okita dying on the bridge with Earth in sight: 「地球か…何もかも、みな懐かしい」, “Earth… how I miss it all.”
It changed
It created the teenage anime audience, the anime boom of 1977 and the word “anime” as everyday Japanese. In the US it became Star Blazers.

1974

CUT {{cut}}TV · 52 episodes

Heidi, Girl of the Alpsアルプスの少女ハイジ

Isao Takahata · scene design Hayao Miyazaki · characters Yōichi Kotabe · Zuiyō

It says
A girl sent to live with her grandfather on the Alm is happiest with mountains, bread and people who know her, and a city house nearly breaks her.
It feels
It is about chores, weather and sleeping in a hayloft, and children watched it every week as if it were an adventure.
It changed
It established the “everyday” realism that runs through Takahata and Miyazaki's later work, and became a staple of childhood across Europe and the Arab world.

1974

CUT {{cut}}TV · 43 episodes

Mobile Suit Gundam機動戦士ガンダム

Yoshiyuki Tomino · characters Yoshikazu Yasuhiko · mecha Kunio Okawara · Sunrise

It says
Teenagers on both sides are turned into soldiers, and Amuro's masked enemy becomes his mirror. The only hope it offers is that people might one day understand each other.
It feels
Life aboard the warship is cramped and tense, the refugees are always in the way, and the masked enemy has his own grief. Amuro is very good at fighting and hates it.
It changed
It created the real-robot genre and a franchise still running nearly fifty years later. By 2024 Bandai had shipped more than 800 million Gundam model kits.

CUT {{cut}}Feature · 129 min

Galaxy Express 999銀河鉄道999

Rintaro · from Leiji Matsumoto's manga · Toei

It says
Tetsurō crosses the galaxy to get a free machine body, and learns on the way that a limited life is the only one worth having.
It feels
Each stop is a new planet and a short story, usually a sad one, and the train always leaves on time. The whole journey is a long goodbye.
It changed
It put Yoshinori Kanada's explosive effects animation in front of a mass audience and made the train an anime symbol of passage between worlds and stages of life.

CUT {{cut}}Feature · 100 min

The Castle of Cagliostroルパン三世 カリオストロの城

Hayao Miyazaki's first feature · Tokyo Movie Shinsha

It says
Lupin rescues a princess from a counterfeiting count, refuses to take her with him, and leaves with nothing but her gratitude.
It feels
A tiny Fiat drives up a cliff face, Lupin runs across rooftops, and the climax plays out inside the gears of a clock tower. The jokes are all in the physics.
It changed
It did only modest business at first, then became a touchstone for animators everywhere and the calling card that let Miyazaki make Nausicaä.

Tadao Nagahama, then Osamu Dezaki · from Riyoko Ikeda's manga · TMS

It says
A woman raised as a man to lead the royal guard chooses the Revolution over the Queen she loves.
It feels
Whenever the story turns, Dezaki freezes the frame and roses drift across it. You know how the Revolution ends, and it makes you watch anyway.

It says
Giant warriors who have known nothing but war are undone by a pop song, and by the culture that comes with it.

1982

波紋

Ripples

In Japan

  • Fandom built its own institutions: Comic Market, specialist magazines, fan clubs, model kits, soundtrack albums and voice-actor stars.
  • Fans became professionals, and the studio Gainax grew out of a convention film.
  • The direct-to-video OVA, from 1983, let studios make work for fans alone.

Around the world

This is the decade anime went abroad in force, mostly as robots. Grendizer became Goldorak in France and Goldrake in Italy in 1978 and swept the Arab world from 1979. Heidi, Candy Candy and Lady Oscar filled European schedules. In the US, Battle of the Planets (1978) and Star Blazers (1979) were heavily rewritten. See the map below for where it landed.

One story

The “martial law babies” of the Philippines

On 27 August 1979 the Marcos government pulled Voltes V, a show about an uprising against a tyrant, four episodes before its end. Viewers who had been children then waited twenty years. The final episodes were not broadcast until 1999.
