Reel 07

2000 — 2010

The Digital Spirit

Digital tools ended the cel era, and the world's top film prizes went to a hand-drawn Japanese fantasy. Meanwhile one man with a desktop computer, and fans online, showed that anime no longer needed a big studio or a broadcaster to reach the world.

デジタル

20 July 2001

Spirited Away opens in Japan. It will take more than ¥30 billion at home and hold the national box-office record for nineteen years.

February 2002

It shares the Golden Bear at the Berlin Film Festival. The same month, Makoto Shinkai previews Voices of a Distant Star, a 25-minute film he made almost entirely alone.

23 March 2003

Spirited Away wins the Oscar for Best Animated Feature. Its director stays home.

May 2006

A site called Crunchyroll launches, built on fan-uploaded video. Within a few years it will become the largest legal anime streamer outside Japan.

24 August 2010

Satoshi Kon dies of pancreatic cancer at 46, leaving a farewell letter titled “Sayonara”.

背景

The world around it

Around the turn of the century Japanese studios finished replacing painted cels with digital ink and paint and computer compositing. Lines became cleaner, colour gradients and lighting effects cheaper, and 3D models began to appear among the drawings. The [[satsuei|compositing]] department, once the people who photographed cels, became the place where much of a show's atmosphere was made.

The decade's defining film was hand-drawn. Spirited Away (2001) follows ten-year-old Chihiro, whose parents turn into pigs at a spirit world's banquet and who must work in a bathhouse for gods to save them. In Japan it earned ¥30.8 billion on first release. It shared the Golden Bear at Berlin with Bloody Sunday in 2002, and in 2003 won the Academy Award for Best Animated Feature, the first for a non-English-language film. Miyazaki did not go to the ceremony. Years later he told the Los Angeles Times: “The reason I wasn't here for the Academy Award was because I didn't want to visit a country that was bombing Iraq.” His next film, Howl's Moving Castle (2004), placed a pointless war at the centre of a story that, in the original novel, had little of one.

Directors flourished. Satoshi Kon followed Perfect Blue with Millennium Actress (2001), Tokyo Godfathers (2003), the TV series Paranoia Agent (2004) and Paprika (2006). Mamoru Oshii's Ghost in the Shell 2: Innocence became, in 2004, the first anime to compete for the Palme d'Or at Cannes. Masaaki Yuasa's Mind Game (2004) exploded animation into collage. Mamoru Hosoda made The Girl Who Leapt Through Time (2006) and Summer Wars (2009). And Makoto Shinkai, a former game-company employee, wrote, directed, animated and edited Voices of a Distant Star over seven months on a Power Mac G4, voicing the first version himself with his partner.

Shōnen series built a global audience: Naruto (2002), Bleach (2004), Fullmetal Alchemist (2003) and its remake Brotherhood (2009), Death Note (2006). Late-night television grew crowded. Kyoto Animation's The Melancholy of Haruhi Suzumiya (2006) spread online through fans copying its closing dance, and its Lucky Star (2007) sent fans on [[seichi]] to Washinomiya Shrine, whose town in 2008 registered the show's fictional sisters as residents. Gainax's Gurren Lagann (2007) and the meditative Mushishi (2005) showed the range.

The internet changed distribution before the industry did. Fans subtitled episodes within hours and shared them through BitTorrent; sites like Crunchyroll, founded in 2006 on fan uploads, moved to licensed streaming as Japanese companies saw the audience and started selling to it. The government noticed too. In 2008 the foreign ministry named Doraemon Japan's “anime ambassador”, and in 2010 the economy ministry set up an office to promote what was being called Cool Japan.

意図・内面

What they reached for, and how it felt

Intent. Miyazaki wanted to make a film for ten-year-old girls, who he felt were offered little but romance stories. He gave Chihiro no special powers, only the will to work and to remember her name. Behind the fantasy sits a Japan that had lost itself in the bubble: the theme park ruin, the parents gorging, No-Face trying to buy affection with gold. Satoshi Kon wanted to show how memory, film and fantasy overlap, and in Millennium Actress he made a woman's whole life into one chase through the history of Japanese cinema. Shinkai wanted to show how ordinary light, a convenience store at night or a train platform at dusk, can break your heart. Imaishi's Gurren Lagann wanted to make you shout.

Interiority. The decade's great films are about holding on to a self. Chihiro forgets her name and almost forgets her parents. The most remembered scene in the film is the quietest: a train ride across a flooded plain, Chihiro and No-Face sitting side by side as stations slide past, a passage of pure [[ma]]. 5 Centimeters per Second (2007) takes its title from the speed at which cherry blossoms fall, and follows a boy whose first love becomes a longing he carries into a numb adulthood. Haruhi's heroine is so bored by ordinary life that she may be rewriting the universe to make it interesting. Fullmetal Alchemist's brothers pay for one act of grief, trying to bring back their dead mother, with their bodies, and spend the series atoning.

2009

波紋

Ripples

In Japan

  • Otaku went from suspect to marketable: the 2005 TV drama Densha Otoko made a fan its romantic hero, and Akihabara became a tourist sight.
  • Anime tourism began in earnest with Lucky Star's shrine.
  • The state adopted anime as soft power: Doraemon as ambassador (2008), Cool Japan (2010).

Around the world

Spirited Away won the West's top prizes. Naruto and Death Note became shared adolescence across continents. Conventions grew from fan meetings into mass events, and fansubbing communities, operating outside the law, built an audience that the industry would soon serve through streaming.

One story

An empty seat at the Oscars

When Spirited Away won in March 2003, days after the invasion of Iraq began, Miyazaki was not in the room. At the time the studio said he was busy. He later said he had refused to visit a country that was bombing Iraq.
