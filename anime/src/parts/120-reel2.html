背景

The world around it

The surrender of August 1945 left the animators who had worked on propaganda with no clients and, under the American Occupation, a new set of rules. Censors discouraged feudal and militaristic themes, and animation survived on advertising, educational films and short commissions. In January 1948 a group of veterans, among them Kenzō Masaoka and Sanae Yamamoto, founded Nihon Dōga in the ruins of Tokyo. It was renamed Nichidō Eiga in 1952. Meanwhile Hollywood's backlog arrived: Disney's Snow White, held back by the war, opened in Japan in 1950, and Japanese artists studied its fluid, fully drawn motion with envy.

In 1956 the film company Toei bought Nichidō and renamed it Tōei Dōga. Its president, Hiroshi Ōkawa, gave it an explicit ambition: to be “the Disney of the East”, Japan's first company built to make full-length theatrical animation, with features that could be sold in Asia and the West. Toei built a new studio at Ōizumi in western Tokyo and organised it like an animation factory, with key animators, in-betweeners and departments of tracers and painters, most of them young women.

The first feature, Hakujaden (The Tale of the White Serpent), came out on 22 October 1958. It adapts the Chinese legend of a white snake spirit who becomes a woman for love of a young man. Directed by Taiji Yabushita, with key animation led by Yasuji Mori and Akira Daikubara, it was Japan's first animated feature in colour. In 1961 it reached American cinemas as Panda and the Magic Serpent. Toei followed with roughly one feature a year: Magic Boy (1959), Saiyūki (1960), adapted from a manga by Osamu Tezuka and sold in the US as Alakazam the Great, and in 1963 The Little Prince and the Eight-Headed Dragon, whose flat, graphic designs and climactic dragon battle, scored by the Godzilla composer Akira Ifukube, are still studied by animators.

The studio's real product turned out to be people. Isao Takahata joined in 1959 and Hayao Miyazaki in 1963. Reiko Okuyama, who applied in 1957 thinking Toei published children's books, was hired as an in-betweener on Hakujaden and went on to become one of the first women in Japan to serve as an animation director. Animators at Toei also organised. Its labour union was active and sometimes fierce, and in 1964 Miyazaki served as its chief secretary, with Takahata beside him. The politics of that union would shape the films they made together.

Elsewhere, the paper animator Noburō Ōfuji remade his silent Kujira (The Whale) in 1952 in coloured cellophane, and it was screened at the Cannes Film Festival in 1953. After his death in 1961 the Mainichi Film Awards created a prize in his name. The Ōfuji Noburō Award is still one of Japan's main honours for animation.

意図・内面

What they reached for, and how it felt

Intent. Toei's founders measured themselves against Disney: full animation, lush colour, music, a story any family could follow. But they chose Asian legends rather than European fairy tales, partly to stand apart and partly to sell in Asia. The animators, led by Yasuji Mori, cared above all about character acting, the small gestures that make a drawn person seem to think. By the early 1960s, with The Little Prince and the Eight-Headed Dragon, the studio was moving away from Disney's round shapes towards flat, stylised design. The search for a Japanese way of animating had begun.

Interiority. Hakujaden is a story of longing across a boundary that should not be crossed: a spirit who gives up her power to be with a human. For an audience that had lived through grey years of war and hunger it offered colour and tenderness on a scale they had not seen in a Japanese film. For one teenager it did more. Miyazaki later wrote that after the film he stumbled home in the snow, ashamed of the cynical manga he had been drawing because it was fashionable, and cried all night. The heroine's sincerity made him want to make something as earnest. It is the feeling this whole history is named after: 感動, being moved, turning into a vocation.

波紋

Ripples

In Japan

Toei became the training ground for the industry. Takahata, Miyazaki, Yasuo Ōtsuka, Yōichi Kotabe and Reiko Okuyama all learned there, and its TV division would later make Mazinger Z, Sailor Moon, Dragon Ball and One Piece. On 31 July 2026 the studio marked seventy years.

Around the world

Export was the plan from the start. Hakujaden, Magic Boy and Alakazam the Great all reached American cinemas by 1961, the first Japanese animated features released there. In June 2026 Toei marked its seventieth year at the Annecy animation festival in France, the industry's biggest international meeting.
