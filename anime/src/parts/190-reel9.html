Reel 09

2020 — 2026

The Whole World Watching

When a pandemic closed the world's doors, anime became its comfort and a common language. By the mid-2020s more of its money came from abroad than from Japan, its films set global box-office records, and one of its symbols flew over protests from Jakarta to Kathmandu.

世界へ

16 October 2020

In half-empty, socially distanced cinemas, Demon Slayer: Mugen Train opens. Seventy-two days later it has passed Spirited Away as the biggest film in Japanese history.

8 March 2021

2024

The world around it背景

Records abroad, strain at home

The pandemic shut cinemas and sent the world indoors, onto streaming services with deep anime libraries. Demand surged. Netflix said in 2025 that more than half of its members worldwide watch anime, about 150 million households, that anime viewing on the service had tripled in five years, and that most of those viewers watch it dubbed. Crunchyroll, bought by Sony in 2021 for $1.175 billion, passed 21 million paying subscribers by March 2026. Episodes now launch simultaneously around the world in dozens of languages.

The money followed the audience. The Association of Japanese Animations put the industry's 2024 revenue at a record ¥3.84 trillion, of which ¥2.17 trillion was earned abroad, the third year in which overseas income exceeded domestic. In 2024 Japan's government set a goal of ¥20 trillion a year in overseas sales for its creative industries by 2033. Box-office records fell repeatedly. Demon Slayer: Mugen Train (2020) took more than ¥40 billion in Japan. Its sequel Infinity Castle (2025) opened to $70.6 million in North America, the biggest debut ever for an anime film, and earned about $820 million worldwide, the most for any Japanese film; at home it became only the second film ever to pass ¥40 billion.

The art kept widening. Hideaki Anno finished Evangelion with 3.0+1.0 Thrice Upon a Time (2021). Takehiko Inoue directed his own The First Slam Dunk (2022) in a hybrid of 3D animation and hand-drawn line. Makoto Shinkai's Suzume (2022) faced the 2011 earthquake directly and competed at the Berlin Film Festival. CloverWorks' Bocchi the Rock! (2022) turned social anxiety into visual comedy, and Madhouse's Frieren (2023) took the top spot of MyAnimeList's rankings from Fullmetal Alchemist: Brotherhood. Kiyotaka Oshiyama's Look Back (2024), an hour-long film about two girls who draw manga, won the Japan Academy Prize. Sources grew more varied too: the Korean webtoon Solo Leveling became a hit anime in 2024, and by September 2026 the year's biggest film in Japan was Chiikawa, adapted from cartoons first posted on social media.

Strains showed. A 2024 survey by the animators' association NAFCA found that more than 40 percent of animators earned under ¥200,000 a month. Japan's Freelance Act, in force from November 2024, gave freelance staff new protections. In 2023 animators on MAPPA's second season of Jujutsu Kaisen said publicly that its schedule had them finishing episodes hours before broadcast, and the argument about how anime is made reached its fans. And anime's influence on the rest of animation became plain. In March 2026 the Oscar for animated feature went to KPop Demon Hunters, an American film whose directors named Sailor Moon and Cowboy Bebop among their models.

How do you live?

Japanese title of Miyazaki's 2023 film, 君たちはどう生きるか, borrowed from Genzaburō Yoshino's 1937 novel for young readers
What they reached for, and how it felt意図・内面

The elders write their endings

Intent. The decade's elders wrote endings. Miyazaki, in his eighties, made The Boy and the Heron about a boy grieving his mother in wartime who is offered the chance to inherit a crumbling world built by his great-uncle, and chooses instead to go back and live in the flawed one. He gave it the title of a book he had read as a child: How Do You Live? Anno ended Evangelion by letting Shinji grow up, and shot the last scene at Ube-Shinkawa station in his own home town. The younger makers were reaching elsewhere. Demon Slayer's Koyoharu Gotōge built a blockbuster on compassion for the defeated. Frieren's writer Kanehito Yamada began a fantasy where others end, after the hero has won, to ask what the journey meant. Tatsuki Fujimoto's Look Back asks why anyone keeps drawing after loss.

Interiority. This was the era when audiences most visibly used anime to feel together. Japanese cinemas filled with people in masks crying at Mugen Train's ending, and its fallen hero's motto, 「心を燃やせ」, “set your heart ablaze”, became a phrase of the pandemic. Bocchi the Rock! made social anxiety funny without mocking it, and many viewers said they had never seen their own inner life drawn so precisely. Frieren moved millions with grief in slow motion: an elf who, decades after her companion's death, realises she never really tried to know him while he was alive, and sets out to understand people. For a generation of young protesters, the Straw Hat flag held a whole inner world: the loyalty of a found family, and contempt for a corrupt world government.

Demon Slayer: Mugen Train劇場版「鬼滅の刃」無限列車編

Haruo Sotozaki · ufotable

Picking up straight after the TV series, Tanjirō, Zenitsu and Inosuke board a night train on which dozens of passengers have vanished. They are to join Kyōjurō Rengoku, the Flame Hashira, one of the corps' strongest swordsmen. Soon after the conductor clips their tickets they fall asleep, and each finds himself in the dream he most wants to be true.

It says
Rengoku believes the strong are born to protect the weak, and gives everything to keep three boys and a trainload of passengers alive until dawn.
It feels
It opens inside a dream that curdles into a nightmare, and ends with a farewell that left cinemas in tears.
It changed
It became the biggest film ever released in Japan, in the middle of a pandemic, and showed how a TV series could feed a theatrical blockbuster.
How it was made

The film was announced the night the first TV season ended, and adapts the manga's Mugen Train arc closely. Ufotable built the train in 3D and drew the characters over it by hand, and painted the dreams in soft, sunlit colours that make waking up feel like a loss. It opened in October 2020, when Japan's cinemas had reopened but Hollywood had postponed nearly everything; with almost no competition it played on screen after screen, and many people saw it more than once.

Why it lasts

What people remember is Rengoku. In two hours the film turns a minor figure from the series into one of the best-loved characters in anime, through little more than his cheerfulness, his appetite and the way he treats three boys he has only just met. His command to “set your heart ablaze”, 心を燃やせ, became a catchphrase.

Look forTanjirō's dream, in which the family he lost is alive, and what he has to do to wake up from it.

Evangelion: 3.0+1.0 Thrice Upon a Timeシン・エヴァンゲリオン劇場版

Hideaki Anno · Studio Khara

The fourth and last of the Rebuild of Evangelion films, which retell the 1995 TV series before breaking away from it. After the catastrophe at the end of the previous film, Shinji Ikari wanders, mute with guilt, with Asuka and a copy of Rei, until they reach a village of survivors where people farm, bathe and raise children. There he must decide whether to face his father one last time.

It says
Shinji and his father finally talk, the children grow up, and the world is allowed to be ordinary: goodbye, all of Evangelion.
It feels
After two and a half hours of noise, the animation thins into storyboards and then into live-action footage of a real town, and the ending feels like being let go.
It changed
It closed one of anime's great open wounds, took ¥10.28 billion, and was read as Anno's own recovery.
How it was made

The TV series had ended in a famously abstract pair of episodes, and its 1997 film in apocalypse. Anno began retelling the story in 2007. After the third film, in 2012, he fell into a depression so deep he could not go near his own studio; he recovered partly by co-directing Shin Godzilla (2016). The last film was then delayed twice by the pandemic. Its tagline read さらば、すべてのエヴァンゲリオン。 Goodbye, all of Evangelion.

After Q was released, I broke. I fell into what is called a depressive state.

Hideaki Anno, writing on Studio Khara's website, 2015
Why it lasts

Anno finished the story twenty-six years after he began it, and ended it with kindness. Viewers who had grown up with Shinji finally watched him grow up too. Its last shots, filmed in Ube, Anno's home town, step out of animation altogether.

Look forThe long stay in Village 3, where a copy of Rei learns to plant rice and to say “good morning”: a slow, warm stretch that tells you where the film is going.

Bocchi the Rock!ぼっち・ざ・ろっく！

Keiichirō Saitō · CloverWorks

Hitori Gotō, nicknamed Bocchi (“loner”), is so socially anxious she can barely speak. She has practised the guitar six hours a day for three years and posts brilliant covers online as “guitarhero”, but has never played with another person. Then a drummer, Nijika, finds her on a playground swing and drags her into a band that needs a guitarist tonight.

It says
Hitori is terrified of people and still makes something with them, one rehearsal at a time.
It feels
When Bocchi panics, the show panics with her: stop-motion, live action, glitch art, whatever her mind is doing.
It changed
It turned emotion symbols into a whole visual language for anxiety, and sent its fictional band's album up the real charts.
How it was made

Hamaji named her four heroines after the members of the rock band Asian Kung-Fu Generation: Gotō, Ijichi, Yamada and Kita. Her strip has no continuous plot, so the writer Erika Yoshida built one around the band's first year of gigs. The songs, credited to the fictional Kessoku Band, were written by real songwriters and sung by the voice cast, and the last episode ends with their cover of Asian Kung-Fu Generation's “Rock 'n' Roll, Morning Light Falls on You”.

Why it lasts

It is one of the kindest portraits of social anxiety in any medium: it laughs with Bocchi, never at her, and lets change come slowly, a gig at a time. Her small victories, ordering a drink, handing out a flyer, playing to a nearly empty room, land as hard as any battle.

Look forEpisode 8, the band's first live show, on a typhoon night, to a nearly empty room, until Bocchi's guitar turns it around.

The First Slam DunkTHE FIRST SLAM DUNK

Takehiko Inoue, directing his own manga · Toei Animation, DandeliOn

Shōhoku High, an unfancied team from Kanagawa, plays the national champions, Sannoh, in the second round of the Inter-High tournament. The film plays the whole game, and between possessions tells the story of Ryōta Miyagi, Shōhoku's small, fast point guard, who grew up in Okinawa idolising an older brother who was a basketball star.

It says
A single game holds a whole life: the film retells a famous match through Ryōta, a player still grieving his older brother.
It feels
The players move like real basketball and look like ink drawings, and the last seconds of the game are almost silent.
It changed
It became one of the most-watched Japanese films ever in South Korea, and showed 3D could carry a mangaka's line.
How it was made

Inoue wrote and directed it himself, twenty-six years after the manga ended. Professional basketball players were filmed in motion capture, and the 3D figures built from their movements were reworked until they looked like Inoue's drawings, so that the games could move like real basketball without losing his line. The off-court scenes were drawn by hand.

Why it lasts

Instead of retelling the story of the manga's hero, Hanamichi Sakuragi, it centres the Shōhoku player readers knew least, and finds a story of grief inside a game they already knew by heart. That longtime fans wept at a result they had known for twenty-six years is the film's achievement.

Look forThe opening titles: the five Shōhoku starters sketched into being in pencil, line by line, as they walk towards us to The Birthday's “Love Rockets”.

Suzumeすずめの戸締まり

Makoto Shinkai · CoMix Wave Films · competed at Berlin, 2023

Suzume, seventeen, lives with her aunt in a seaside town in Kyushu. On her way to school she meets a young man looking for ruins, and follows him to an abandoned hot-spring resort, where a door stands alone in the rubble. She opens it. Sōta, it turns out, is a “closer”, from a family who have kept such doors shut for generations; before long he is trapped in a child's three-legged chair, and Suzume sets off across Japan to help him.

It says
Disasters leave doors open, and Suzume, orphaned by the 2011 tsunami, travels Japan closing them until she faces her own.
It feels
A road trip with a talking three-legged chair ends on the Tōhoku coast, where Suzume meets the child she was.
It changed
It was the most direct treatment of 3.11 in mainstream anime, and the first Japanese animated film in competition at Berlin since Spirited Away.
How it was made

The journey runs from Kyushu through Shikoku and Kobe to Tokyo and on to the Tōhoku coast, through places emptied by a shrinking population or struck by earthquakes. Before its release the distributor warned audiences that the film contains earthquake scenes and the alarm Japanese phones sound before a tremor. The chair Sōta is trapped in was made for Suzume by her mother, who died in the tsunami of 2011.

Why it lasts

Behind the road-movie fun is a careful act of public mourning. To close each door, Suzume must remember the people who once lived there and hear their voices on an ordinary morning, setting off for the day. The film asks a generation too young to remember 2011 to do the same.

Look forThe chair. Sōta spends most of the film as a three-legged child's chair, and the animators give it a whole performance: running, sulking, falling asleep.

The Boy and the Heron君たちはどう生きるか

Hayao Miyazaki · Studio Ghibli · Golden Globe and Academy Award, 2024

During the Pacific War, a boy, Mahito, loses his mother in a hospital fire in Tokyo. His father, who makes canopies for fighter planes, marries her younger sister and moves them to her family's country estate. There a grey heron begins to taunt Mahito, telling him his mother is still alive, and leads him to a sealed tower built by an eccentric great-uncle around a fallen meteor.

It says
Mahito is offered a perfect world to inherit and chooses the damaged one, where he can live with friends.
It feels
It feels like walking through Miyazaki's memory, past his wartime childhood and images from his earlier films.
It changed
The first anime to win a Golden Globe. It won Miyazaki his second Oscar, twenty-one years after the first.
How it was made

Miyazaki announced his retirement after The Wind Rises in 2013, and within a few years was back at his desk. The film took seven years. Its Japanese title comes from Genzaburō Yoshino's 1937 novel How Do You Live?, which Mahito finds and reads. Ghibli's producer, Toshio Suzuki, released it with no trailer, no stills and a single poster, so audiences in Japan went in knowing nothing.

Why it matters

Read beside Miyazaki's life, it comes close to autobiography: like Mahito, Miyazaki was a wartime boy whose family's firm made parts for fighter planes. And the great-uncle, an old man who has built a world and must decide who will inherit it, is hard not to see as Miyazaki himself.

Look forThe opening fire, drawn as a smear of heat, crowds and distorted bodies, unlike anything in Miyazaki's earlier work.

Frieren: Beyond Journey's End葬送のフリーレン

Keiichirō Saitō · Madhouse · season 2, January–March 2026

The story begins where most fantasies end. A hero's party has defeated the Demon King after a ten-year quest, and comes home in triumph. One of them is Frieren, an elf mage who will live for more than a thousand years, for whom the journey was a brief episode. Fifty years later she returns to see the hero, Himmel, now an old man. At his funeral she weeps, and realises she never really got to know him.

It says
Frieren, who will live for millennia, understands only after her companions are gone that the time spent with them was the treasure.
It feels
Frieren understands how much Himmel meant to her only at his funeral, and the series takes its time letting the memories arrive.
It changed
It displaced Fullmetal Alchemist: Brotherhood at the top of MyAnimeList, and made the [[mononoaware|elegiac]] fantasy a mass-market hit.
How it was made

Yamada and Abe's manga began in Weekly Shōnen Sunday in 2020. Madhouse gave the adaptation to Keiichirō Saitō, fresh from Bocchi the Rock!, and it premiered in an unprecedented slot: four episodes in a row on Nippon TV's Friday Road Show, the feature-film programme where Japanese families watch Ghibli films, before moving to late nights. Its magic is unusually quiet: Frieren collects obscure, everyday spells, and prizes them above battle magic.

Why it matters

Episodes skip years in a single cut, and the smallest moments, a ring given, a flower planted, a meal shared, return decades later with their full weight. Slow and quiet as it is, it overtook Fullmetal Alchemist: Brotherhood at the top of MyAnimeList's user rankings.

Look forEpisode 2, in which Frieren spends months searching for a single kind of flower, because Himmel once wanted to show it to her.

Look Backルックバック

Kiyotaka Oshiyama · Studio Durian · from Tatsuki Fujimoto's one-shot manga

Fujino, a fourth-grader in a small country town, draws four-panel comics for the school newspaper and is used to being the best. Then the paper starts printing strips by Kyomoto, a girl who never comes to school, and they are better than hers. Stung, Fujino practises for years. When the two finally meet, Kyomoto turns out to be her biggest fan.

It says
Fujino keeps drawing for the person who first believed in her work, even after losing her.
It feels
It keeps the wobble of the pencil lines, and when Fujino skips home through the rain you can feel the hands that drew her.
It changed
An hour-long film from a new studio, it was a box-office surprise and won the Japan Academy Prize for animation.
How it was made

Fujimoto, the creator of Chainsaw Man, published the 143-page story on Shueisha's free app Shōnen Jump+ in July 2021, where it became a sensation overnight. Oshiyama, a veteran animator and the director of Flip Flappers (2016), adapted it at his small Studio Durian and kept the marks of the pencil: many shots keep the rough lines of the drawings instead of cleaning them up. Released as a special screening, it was sold at one flat price with no discounts, and still took more than ¥2 billion.

Why it matters

In under an hour it covers years of two lives and asks why anyone keeps drawing, even after loss. It has struck a particular chord with people who draw for a living.

Look forThe run home in the rain after the first compliment. It is pure 感動 in a single shot.

Demon Slayer: Infinity Castle劇場版「鬼滅の刃」無限城編 第一章

Haruo Sotozaki · ufotable · first of a trilogy

The first of three films adapting the manga's final arc. Muzan Kibutsuji, the first demon, drops the demon slayers into his fortress, an endless, shifting maze of rooms, corridors and staircases, where each must face one of his strongest demons. Tanjirō, now a seasoned fighter, finds himself face to face with the demon he most wants to defeat.

It says
Even at the climax of the war, the film stops to mourn how its demons were made.
It feels
Rooms and staircases rotate around the fighters without end, built in 3D and painted to look hand-made, so the castle itself feels like an enemy.
It changed
The biggest worldwide release a Japanese film has ever had: about $820 million, and the first to pass ¥100 billion globally.
How it was made

Ufotable built the castle as a 3D set, so that the camera can fall through it with the fighters, and drew the characters over it by hand. It opened in Japan in July 2025 and in North America in September, where it took $70.6 million on its first weekend: the biggest opening there for any anime film, and for any film not in English.

Why it matters

It is the clearest sign yet of how far anime's audience has grown. The first part of the finale to a TV saga of more than sixty episodes, it opened at number one in the United States, carried there by viewers who had followed the whole story on streaming services.

Look forThe long flashback in the film's last act. It changes what the final fight means.

2025

波紋

Ripples

In Japan

  • Anime became national economic policy, with a ¥20 trillion export target for 2033.
  • Studios faced a production crunch: more shows, too few animators, low pay, and new legal protections for freelancers.
  • The Association of Japanese Animations issued guidance on generative AI in 2025.

Around the world

Anime is now global popular culture. More than half of Netflix's members watch it. A Japanese pop song, YOASOBI's “Idol” from Oshi no Ko, topped Billboard's Global chart outside the US in 2023, the first Japanese-language song to do so. Saudi, Korean, American and Chinese companies co-produce it, and Western animation borrows its language openly.

One story

Rohan Rai, nineteen, Kathmandu

In September 2025 young Nepalis marched under a cartoon skull in a straw hat. Asked what the One Piece flag meant to him, Rai told NPR it stood for “freedom, loyalty and defiance against injustice”.

Also essential

Jujutsu Kaisen呪術廻戦

2020 · TV, from 2020 · Sunghoo Park · MAPPA

Yūji Itadori, a high-schooler of absurd strength, swallows the rotting finger of an ancient demon to save his friends, and becomes the vessel of Sukuna, the King of Curses. Sentenced to death, he is reprieved by his teacher, Satoru Gojō: first he must find and eat all twenty fingers. MAPPA's fights raised the bar for TV action, and the prequel film Jujutsu Kaisen 0 took ¥13.8 billion in Japan.

Spy × FamilySPY×FAMILY

2022 · TV, from 2022 · Kazuhiro Furuhashi · Wit Studio, CloverWorks

A spy codenamed Twilight needs a fake family to get close to a target, so he adopts a little girl and marries a woman he barely knows. He does not know that the girl, Anya, can read minds, or that his wife is an assassin. A comedy of three people keeping secrets from one another, it became that rare thing, an anime whole families watch together.

Chainsaw Manチェンソーマン

2022 · TV, 12 episodes · Ryū Nakayama · MAPPA

Denji is so poor he has sold his own organs to pay his dead father's debts to the yakuza, hunting devils with his chainsaw-dog, Pochita. When he is betrayed and killed, Pochita becomes his heart, and he becomes Chainsaw Man. MAPPA paid for the series entirely itself, without the usual [[seisakuiinkai|production committee]], and gave each of its twelve episodes a different ending song by a different artist.

Cyberpunk: Edgerunnersサイバーパンク エッジランナーズ

2022 · Netflix, 10 episodes · Hiroyuki Imaishi · Studio Trigger

Set in the world of the Polish video game Cyberpunk 2077, it follows David, a street kid in Night City who installs a military-grade implant and joins a crew of mercenaries. Studio Trigger, founded by veterans of Gurren Lagann, gave the game's grim city speed, neon and heartbreak in ten episodes. It won Anime of the Year at the 2023 Crunchyroll Anime Awards.

Oshi no Ko【推しの子】

2023 · TV, from 2023 · Daisuke Hiramaki · Doga Kobo

A country doctor devoted to a teenage idol is murdered and reborn as one of her secret twins. Aka Akasaka and Mengo Yokoyari's manga turns that outrageous premise into a hard look at Japan's entertainment industry. The first episode ran 82 minutes and opened in cinemas, and its theme, YOASOBI's “Idol”, became the first Japanese-language song to top Billboard's Global chart outside the US.

PlutoPLUTO

2023 · Netflix, 8 episodes · Toshio Kawaguchi · Studio M2

Someone is destroying the seven most advanced robots in the world, and Gesicht, the Europol detective on the case, is a robot and one of the seven. Naoki Urasawa and Takashi Nagasaki rebuilt “The Greatest Robot on Earth”, a 1964 Astro Boy story by Osamu Tezuka, as a murder mystery haunted by a war fought over weapons that were never found. Studio M2's eight hour-long episodes were announced in 2017 and reached Netflix in 2023.

Dandadanダンダダン

2024 · TV, from 2024 · Fūga Yamashiro · Science SARU

Momo believes in ghosts but not aliens; Okarun, the classmate she rescues from bullies, believes in aliens but not ghosts. To settle it, each visits the other's haunt, and both turn out to be right. Science SARU's adaptation of Yukinobu Tatsu's manga is screwball comedy, teen romance and monster fight at once, and its seventh episode, a ghost's backstory, was hailed as one of the year's best.

Solo Leveling俺だけレベルアップな件

2024 · TV, from 2024 · Shunsuke Nakashige · A-1 Pictures

Gates to monster-filled dungeons open without warning, and Sung Jinwoo is the weakest of the hunters who clear them, until a near-fatal raid makes him the only person who can level up, like a character in a game. Adapted from Chugong's Korean web novel and its hugely popular webtoon, it showed how far anime now draws on all of Asia, and won Anime of the Year at the 2025 Crunchyroll Anime Awards.

Chainsaw Man – The Movie: Reze Arcレゼ篇

2025 · Feature, 100 min · Tatsuya Yoshihara · MAPPA

Denji, now a devil hunter, falls for Reze, a girl who works in a café and shelters with him from the rain in a phone booth. The film adapts one of the best-loved arcs of Fujimoto's manga, a summer romance that turns violent. It opened at number one in North America in October 2025, ahead of Hollywood's new releases, with $17.25 million.
